What he promised vs. what he's sponsored

Sawyer's 2024 campaign named housing his explicit top priority. In a Salt Lake Tribune commentary, he wrote he'd "fight for policies that encourage responsible development and remove unnecessary regulations that drive up the cost of building homes," and told the Standard-Examiner directly: "My top priority will be lowering the cost of housing." He also pledged to prioritize education — properly funding schools, raising teacher pay, cutting administrative burden, expanding local control.

His full sponsored-bill record across two sessions shows he chief-sponsored one bill that shares "real estate" language with his campaign promise — but on closer reading, it isn't housing policy. HB 395, "Real Estate Transaction Amendments," actually enacts the "Utah Real Estate Cybercrime Prevention Act": a wire-fraud/cybersecurity measure requiring everyone in a closing (appraisers, escrow agents, mortgage lenders, title agents, real estate agents) to route transaction communications through one state-sanctioned network, and creating a new 5-member state board to select and regulate the private company that runs it. It was filed and never passed. His only bill that actually touches housing affordability is a second one, "Rental Dwelling Amendments," which he abandoned before it was ever formally introduced. His other chief-sponsored bills — HB 493 (Consumer Products Amendments, signed, a retail shelf-pricing accuracy law), HB 267 (Public Employee Compensation Amendments, filed, not passed), and HB 100 (Electroconvulsive Therapy Prohibition Amendments, filed, not passed) — are unrelated to housing or education. The actual 2026 housing legislation that did pass — HB 492 (infrastructure fund), HB 436 (Moderate Income Housing Infrastructure), HB 68 — was carried by other representatives. On education, his only connection is as one of several cosponsors (not chief sponsor) on Rep. Pierucci's teacher-mentoring bill, HB 146 (signed).

A fourth promise, also unfulfilled so far: on 11/14/2024, before taking office, Sawyer signed U.S. Term Limits' Article V convention pledge — a written commitment to "cosponsor, vote for, and defend" a state resolution applying for a federal constitutional convention limited to congressional term limits. No term-limits or Article V convention resolution appears anywhere in his sponsored, floor-sponsored, or cosponsored bill record across two sessions.

The absence pattern

Two fully-verified findings from Sawyer's committee record, cross-checked against primary-source minutes for every meeting cited:

  • Voting record: Sawyer voted yes on essentially everything through October 2025, then went unrecorded/absent on a cluster of consumer-protection, insurance-code, and trust-business bills starting in November 2025 — immediately after a same-day donation cluster on 9/4/25 from Utah Apartment Association PAC, Utah Bankers Association, and Utah Property Rights Coalition. At the November 19 meeting specifically, he voted through item 9, then was absent for every remaining roll call through adjournment.
  • Judicial stack: Sawyer voted yes on both S.B. 134 and H.B. 392, the two flagship 2026 judicial-control bills already documented in Kyle's HD8 file — the second Weber County Republican confirmed on the core stack.
Sawyer & Co — a brand-new entity, timed with his own disclosure update

Sawyer's most recent COI filing lists his current employer as "Sawyer & Co," a "client acquisition company" he owns — but the same form's Item 2A (entities he owns/officers) is marked "n/a," directly contradicting Item 1B on the same document. Utah's corporate registry shows Sawyer & Co, LLC was formed 7/8/2026, weeks before this COI update, registered at Sawyer's own home address, with a stated business purpose of "Lead Generation for service businesses." Its second listed member, James Weldon Neff, has no other apparent connection to Sawyer — his only other Utah corporate history is Key Concierge, LLC, a Draper company inactive since 2011. Neff isn't using an address of his own on the Sawyer & Co filing either; it lists Sawyer's home address.

Choberka's record

Choberka is currently Ogden City Council Chair for District 1 — a sitting local official running for the state seat — and also works as a Select Health "Program Manager, Equity Initiatives" and a Weber State adjunct English professor. Her COI disclosure is clean: no owned entities, no $5K+ stock holdings, no undisclosed outside income. Board seats: Lantern House (Secretary), the Weber-Morgan Health Department Board of Health, the Weber Local Homeless Trust, and the MLK Jr. Human Rights Commission (a Cox gubernatorial appointee).

2024 (her first, losing run): $88,966.94 raised. Top donors: Utah House Democratic Leadership Council $7,000, Utah Women in Politics PAC $5,000, IBEW PAC $4,000, George & Mary Hall $3,000 (funding her again in 2026), Roxane Googin $1,270 (the same Park City donor tied to UINPAC's own founding). Her September 30th Report shows one confirmed, publicly-visible [DELETED] entry: originally a $500 line item to "The Monarch Venues" (9/25/24), wiped via amendment on 9/30/24 — date, name, purpose, and amount all removed.

2025: zero contributions raised all year — a fundraising-dormant year, with only routine platform fees and small community-goodwill gifts (Youth Impact, Boys & Girls Club, Ogden Contemporary Art, the Innocence Project) on the ledger.

2026 cycle-to-date: $33,949.59 raised. Received the same $7,500 UINPAC gift as Kim James, same day, part of the documented multi-candidate Democratic slate. Heavy consultant spend — Elevate Strategies ($8,755.29) and Apollo Political ($6,939.00) together are over 70% of 2026 spending, a professionally-run operation distinct from a DIY campaign. Cross-Democratic-slate giving continues: David and Rosemary Lesser combined $2,500, and a mutual-support pattern with Bianca Mittendorf's HD7 campaign (each gave the other's campaign a small direct gift).
