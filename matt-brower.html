Heber City Manager · Hired, Not Elected

The Board Seat That Never Came Up

Matt Brower runs Heber City's day-to-day government — a job no voter gave him. He also sat on the board of the nonprofit his own city hired without a bid. The minutes from the meeting that approved it record zero conflicts disclosed.

What CAMS is

Community Alliance for Main Street (CAMS) is a Heber City nonprofit focused on downtown revitalization — organizing events, beautification, and promotion for Main Street businesses. Founded in 2003 by Tom Stone and Jeff Bradshaw using a $15,000 Winter Olympics community grant, it went dormant during the 2008 recession and was revived around 2018. It isn't a city department; it's a separate nonprofit that Heber City pays, under contract, to do this work. Rachel Kahler is its paid Executive Director. Its board of directors — the volunteers who oversee the organization — has included Matt Brower, Dallin Koecher, Tom Stone, and several other Heber-area officials and businesspeople.

Matt Brower has been Heber City Manager since July 2018, appointed and supervised by the City Council, not elected by voters. As city manager, he oversees city operations and finances and carries out the council's policy — real authority over a growing city's budget, with no ballot ever involved.

The CAMS seat

Brower is also a director of Community Alliance for Main Street (CAMS) since 2019, and of Red White & Blue Festival Inc. since 2023 — the same boards Rachel Kahler (CAMS's paid Executive Director) and Dallin Koecher also sit on. In early 2026, the city he manages hired CAMS on a three-year, $39,900-a-year contract to run downtown events, without a competitive bidding process. The city's own mechanism for skipping that process was sole source procurement — a legal exception that, under Utah law, only applies when a requirement is genuinely available from just one supplier, requires a written justification submitted for approval in advance, and requires public notice if the total contract value exceeds $50,000. This three-year contract totals $119,700.

Brower's board seat had been disclosed nowhere in the minutes of the meeting that approved it. The Jan. 20, 2026 Heber City Council minutes record, in Section V, "Conflict of Interest Disclosure":

"There were no conflicts disclosed."

— Official minutes, Heber City Council, Jan. 20, 2026 — the same meeting that approved the CAMS contract

The item itself received no separate discussion; it was folded into a broader motion covering three unrelated agenda items and passed 5-0. The city's defense, later, was that CAMS's board — including Brower's own name — appeared in a staff report attached to the meeting packet, even if it wasn't raised aloud. That packet is a real document, but it isn't the same thing as a disclosure, and it isn't reflected anywhere in the minutes themselves.

A complaint over exactly this — Brower's undisclosed board seat, and the city skipping competitive bidding before hiring CAMS — was filed with the Utah State Auditor's office. It's the only complaint anywhere in this cluster of Heber officials that has actually reached a state oversight body.

A contentious contract, this spring

In April 2026, with his contract set to expire, Brower asked the council for three more years. What was slated as a five-minute vote turned into weeks of delay: a public petition against renewal drew more than 360 signatures, a special meeting ran a three-hour closed session before the public portion even began, and dozens of residents spoke — most of them critical.

Reporting on the delay cited several specific concerns feeding the council's hesitation: a financial audit of Lincoln, California — a city Brower had managed years earlier, where state auditors later found improper handling of public funds and practices that masked budget deficits — the council's own failure to give Brower regular performance evaluations, and a complaint made about Brower to the Utah State Auditor's office. That complaint is almost certainly the same one tied to his CAMS seat.

The council voted 4-2 on April 14, 2026 to renew his contract through May 2029. Mayor Heidi Franco and Councilmember Yvonne Barney voted no.

Where this stands

Brower was not asked for comment, consistent with this publication's practice of reporting from public records and documents in hand. He was never elected to the position that gives him this authority — the City Council hired him, supervises him, and just voted to keep him three more years.
