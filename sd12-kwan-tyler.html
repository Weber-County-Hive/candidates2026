Five Things the Records Show

IN PLAIN WORDSTwo people are running for this state Senate seat. One is a longtime lawmaker with a large, mostly small-donor campaign. The other reports very little money and no stated positions.

  1. Kwan raised $88,651 in cash in 2026. Individuals gave $58,450.00 in 286 entries. PACs, unions and businesses gave $30,201.00 in 28 checks. Her largest is $10,000 from the Senate Democrats’ caucus PAC.
  2. She spent $18,201.01 through the Primary Report. The largest payee is Tissot Solutions ($7,719.31), a fundraising and compliance consulting firm. She also gave $4,934.70 to Democratic groups, another candidate, a school and the Taylorsville Dayzz city festival.
  3. Tyler reports $726 raised and no spending. Her export lists two gifts ($76) on the Primary Report and her later list adds three ($650). No campaign website or stated positions were found.
  4. Two of Kwan’s seven 2026 bills became law, plus three House bills she carried in the Senate. None set money aside. On the Senate floor she voted yes on the data center bill (H.B. 507) and no on the final Inland Port water bill (S.B. 225), which removed a 200,000-gallon cap.
  5. Kwan used the optional part of her disclosure form to say her home sits next to state open space on the Jordan River and that development there “might constitute a conflict.” Tyler answered “n/a” to every item after her job.

How the H.B. 507 committee vote was taken

On March 2, the minutes say “Acting Chair Kwan called the meeting to order.” Three of the committee’s six members voted on H.B. 507; Senate President Adams, Sen. Millner and Sen. Stevenson were absent for that vote. Under Senate Rule SR3-2-203, a majority of a standing committee is a quorum, and leadership members count toward it only while present. With the President absent, the quorum was three, so the quorum rule was met. Speakers in favor were the Utah Association of Counties and the Utah League of Cities and Towns.
