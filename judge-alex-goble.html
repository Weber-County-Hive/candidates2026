Hive judge-rubric grades

How to read the grades: A C means the record meets what Utah law requires, the status quo. A B or A means the judge went beyond it, and the record shows it. D means the judge passed JPEC’s minimum but JPEC rates that area below the judge’s peers; F means below JPEC’s legal minimum score of 3.6. Every grade is taken from JPEC’s own published wording, the same way for all 57 judges. No grade is based on how any case came out, or on who appointed the judge.

No committee for this judge was found as of Oct. 17, 2026. That is not a finding that none exists; it will be checked again after Oct. 27.

What JPEC’s report says

The text below is JPEC’s own public report, reproduced as published. Quotes inside it are from JPEC’s survey respondents and courtroom observers, not from the Hive.

JPEC summary

Survey Respondent Recommendation: 95% of survey respondents recommend Judge Goble for retention.

Performance Measures: Survey scores are analyzed for statistical significance to determine if the judge scored well above, above, consistent with, below, or well below their juvenile court peers.  Above Peers: Integrity & Judicial Temperament, Administrative Skills,

Consistent with Peers: Legal Ability, Procedural Fairness

Respondent and Observer Feedback:Respondents and courtroom observers appreciate Judge Goble’s compassionate, respectful treatment of those in court, with respondents describing him as caring, approachable, and kind.

Both groups speak positively about his willingness to explain information and ensure understanding in court.

Respondents further find the judge to be knowledgeable and prepared, while observers note his neutrality, with one saying that he “grounded decisions in the law rather than personal opinion.”

All four observers report confidence that they would be treated fairly if they were to appear in Judge Goble’s court.

Courtroom observers, overall

  • Together, the four observers were enthusiastically positive about Judge Goble.
  • All observers reported that if they appeared before Judge Goble they would be treated fairly.

What observers agreed on

  • Observers variously reported that Judge Goble exhibited stellar interpersonal skills and courteous behavior, showing “respect and consideration for each individual.” He cared about how outcomes might affect participants and the community and wanted all to succeed, in one case, “[praising]” a youth “who self-reported an unwelcome visit by a friend while on home detention.” He demonstrated concern for participant needs, especially juvenile needs, and, on one matter, he permitted a disposition to take place in a different city “to make it easier for the defendant and family.” The judge appeared detailed, unhurried, and careful, in one instance, asking counsel, “Did I miss anything that I should address with your client?” He invited all to participate, asking about the “situations, goals, and hopes” of defendants, and he seemed skilled at “[building] conversations with people who might be reticent to open up to an authority figure.” He showed clear reasoning, explained rulings and defendants’ rights, and “gave good descriptions of the requirements that litigants had to meet.”
  • All observers particularly emphasized the judge’s neutrality. They described the judge as transparent, rights oriented, and as one who holds participants accountable. One said that he “addressed all litigants with [a] uniform demeanor regardless of age, gender, or status,” and another observed in one matter that he “treated the participant with equal fairness and impartiality.” A third recorded that he “[grounded] decisions in the law rather than personal opinion.”

Other observations

  • Two observers reported that the judge listened and conveyed focus. One said that he was attentive, “[took notes],” and listened, while the other stated that he listened and “participants felt that he was attentive to them.”
  • Two commented positively on the judge’s preparedness. One felt that he was timely and prepared, describing him as “a great switch-hitter who's ready for the full spectrum of curveballs that juvenile court offers.” The second found that he was “clearly prepared for each case.”

Biography (from JPEC)

Judge Alex Goble was appointed to the Sixth District Juvenile Court in August, 2022 by Governor Spencer Cox. He serves Sanpete, Sevier, Piute, Wayne, Garfield and Kane counties. Judge Goble received his Bachelor of Arts degree from Westminster College and his Juris Doctorate from the University of Utah, SJ Quinney College of Law. He was a practicing attorney from 2009 until his appointment. Prior to his appointment, Judge Goble served as the Deputy San Juan County Attorney in the Seventh District and as an Assistant Attorney General in the Child Welfare Division for Grand and San Juan Counties. Before practicing in the Seventh District he served as an Assistant Attorney General in the Criminal Division throughout every District in Utah, and as a prosecutor for Salt Lake City. He has been active with Drug Court programs since 2016 including Family and Adult Drug Court.
