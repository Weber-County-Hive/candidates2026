WASATCH COUNTY COUNCIL — SEAT C · CONTESTED RACE

One Disclosed. One Didn't.

Erik Rowland's own conflict-of-interest form says he has none to report. Alex Folmer, the write-in candidate now challenging him, checked the opposite box on the same county form and named her own.

The race

Erik Rowland has held Wasatch County Council Seat C since January 2023. His bid for a second term was uncontested until the write-in filing period closed Aug. 28, 2026, when Alex Folmer entered the race. The general election is Nov. 3, 2026.

A note on scope: this outlet reviews every candidate's available public record in full. Rowland, a sitting councilmember since 2023 and a May 2026 appointee to the Military Installation Development Authority (MIDA) board, has years of board seats, business filings, and conflict-of-interest disclosures on the public record. Folmer, a first-time write-in candidate, has never held office, so there's no board or voting record to review — but she does have a documented pre-candidacy public record on the exact growth and development questions this race turns on, detailed below. The difference in volume between the two candidates' sections reflects the kind of record each has actually generated, not a decision to scrutinize one candidate more than the other.

Erik Rowland's disclosure record

Rowland's Wasatch County Conflict of Interest Disclosure Form, filed Jan. 2, 2026 and used to cover his county seat as well as his MIDA-linked positions, checks "I have no conflict of interest to report" and leaves every line blank. Independently confirmed against his own LinkedIn profile, his own words to the Park Record, and Utah Division of Corporations filings, at least eight entities and positions don't appear anywhere on that form: a CAMS board seat, ownership/officer roles in JASE Medical / SignalRx LLC and TimeMD Inc., sole membership in Rowland Development LLC, a manager role in Triumph Assurance Group LLC, and seats on the Heber Valley Tourism & Economic Development Board and the North Village SSD Administrative Control Board.

A ninth board — the Wasatch Open Lands Board (WOLB), which reviews conservation-easement applications and recommends how the county's open-space bond money gets spent — has no disclosure form on file for Rowland at all, not even the wrong one. WOLB's own rule bars a member (or their spouse, parent, sibling, or child) from holding a 10%-or-greater interest in any property under consideration for that funding. Rowland owns a development LLC. No filed disclosure means no record of whether that rule was ever checked against it.

The full build of this finding — the MIDA board comparison table, the WOLB gap, the state-law framework requiring a sectioned disclosure form, and the open question of whether the county clerk's own enforcement duty was ever triggered — is in this outlet's original case file, "The Wrong Form, Four Times."

Graded here against Rowland's documented disclosure record specifically — what he actually filed, set against what's independently confirmed and absent from it. All five criteria apply to him because he's held office since 2023; Folmer's card below is graded differently, for reasons explained there.

Alex Folmer

Folmer's own Wasatch County Conflict of Interest Disclosure Form, filed Aug. 31, 2026 for the same Seat C race, checks the opposite box from Rowland's — "I have the following conflict of interest to report" — and lists two items: her employer, the University of Utah, where she works as a Software Design Engineer, and her ownership of Runa Digital, LLC, a Midway-based company. On the disclosure itself, the documented facts hold up: she's independently confirmed as both the registered agent and sole member of Runa Digital, LLC in Utah's own business registry — not just self-reported.

That LLC was formed Feb. 5, 2021 and renewed through Jan. 31, 2024; its renewal lapsed after Feb. 28, 2025, and the state marked it administratively dissolved on July 21, 2026, about five weeks before she signed her disclosure form listing it as a current business interest. Two other LLCs tied to her name in the same registry search — Into the Mystic Mountain LLC and Wild Utah Provisions LLC — are also administratively dissolved, a lapsed-renewal pattern common to solo LLC owners who let side ventures go inactive rather than a sign of anything disclosure-related.

Before filing to run, Folmer has a real public record on the growth and development questions this race centers on. At the Jan. 28, 2026 public hearing on UDOT's Heber Valley Corridor bypass, she testified in person opposing both proposed alternatives, described a 20-year background in tech and data consulting for state agencies, said she had independently reviewed UDOT's own traffic projections and found them overstated, and closed by committing to "continue to make GRAMA requests, pull the data, and get a different perspective on where we are today with this bypass." Weeks earlier, a Feb. 4, 2026 Park Record letter to the editor under her name urged the developer behind the "Slopes" project in Heber Valley to pursue a conservation-easement partnership with a land trust rather than build, citing the valley's agricultural heritage. Both predate her candidacy and are consistent with each other in substance and voice.

No campaign finance record is available for Folmer yet, consistent with a write-in filing that closed only days before her disclosure form was signed.

Folmer's report card

Read this differently than Rowland's grades above. Rowland's D is a score of what he's actually done in office. Folmer's grades below cover only whether her disclosure is honest — they are not a measure of how she'd perform in office, because she isn't in office. A perfect score here means her paperwork checks out, nothing more.

Folmer has never held office, so three of the five criteria — Power, Environmental Impact, and Community Impact — measure the exercise of authority she doesn't have yet, and grading her on them would mean scoring something that doesn't exist. Transparency and Financial Accountability are different: those can be graded right now, against what she actually filed and what's independently confirmed about it.

Power N/A Holds no elected or appointed office. No exercised authority exists yet to grade.
Transparency A Good Checked "I have the following conflict of interest to report" and named both her employer and her business ownership. Both items independently confirmed accurate: the University of Utah campus directory lists her as an IT Software Engineer IV at the same building named on her form, and Utah's business registry confirms her as registered agent and sole member of Runa Digital, LLC.
Financial accountability A Good Disclosed sole ownership of Runa Digital, LLC without qualification. The LLC's state registration lapsed in 2025 after four years active — a separate administrative fact that doesn't change the accuracy of what she reported: she owns it, and she said so.
Environmental impact N/A No vote or funding decision exists yet to grade. Her pre-candidacy public record — a Jan. 28, 2026 EIS hearing comment and a Feb. 4, 2026 Park Record letter, both opposing specific Heber Valley development proposals — is advocacy, not an exercise of governmental authority.
Community impact N/A Same reasoning as environmental impact — nothing decided in office yet to grade for its effect on any community.
A — 2 of 5 graded Not a five-criteria score like Rowland's D above, and not a preview of how she'd govern — it's a grade on two questions only: did she disclose what she owns and where she works, and was it true. Both check out. The other three criteria stay ungraded, not passing, until she either holds office or the race ends without her.
