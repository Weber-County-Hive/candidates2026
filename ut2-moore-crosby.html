Eight Things the Records Show

IN PLAIN WORDSThe incumbent has about 42 times more money than his challenger, and most of it comes from political committees, not people. Most of his accomplishments check out, but one is listed under the wrong year, and several were other members' bills that he cosponsored.

  1. Most of Moore's money comes from committees. His July report shows $1,426,000 from PACs and other political committees and $1,011,572.23 from individuals since the 2024 election: 58.5% committees, 41.5% people. He has not put in any of his own money.
  2. Crosby's money comes from people. He reports $60,224.97, all from individuals except $175.48 of his own. No PAC money, no loans.
  3. Election Hive was paid $150,318.11. Moore's campaign made 14 payments to Election Hive for consulting from February 2025 to May 2026, $69,777.83 of it on May 6, 2026, seven weeks before the primary. Election Hive is the Salt Lake firm the Hive has followed through state campaigns, including Katrina Gibson's in Weber County.
  4. Two more names from other Hive files. Moore's campaign paid Gathering Inc. of Ogden $164,090 for polling and canvassing, and Irish Elk LLC of Layton $86,281.50 for fundraising consulting. Both appear in the Hive's state-level files.
  5. One accomplishment is listed under the wrong year. His website credits his Chinese circuit-board provisions to the 2024 defense bill. His own 2021 press release, the law and a 2026 federal rule all place them in the 2022 defense bill.
  6. Several "laws" were other members' bills. The Great Salt Lake saline-lakes act, the cybercrime metrics act and the Medal of Honor monument became law as bills sponsored by others. Moore sponsored the House version or cosponsored. His page lists them as signed into law without saying so.
  7. The lake program was authorized, not funded. The saline-lakes law allows $5 million a year for 2022 through 2027. The Hive found no spending bill that provided money for that program.
  8. His family's money is mostly in real estate. His 2025 disclosure lists a blind trust ($1–5 million), a Washington rental property ($1–5 million), and his wife's stakes in Boyer family partnerships, each over $1 million, including one tied to The Boyer Company, a commercial developer.

Who Moore is. Born in Ogden in 1980. University of Utah (behavioral science and health) and Northwestern (public policy). He worked in the U.S. Foreign Service and was a principal at Cicero Group, a Salt Lake consulting firm. Before running, he co-chaired Better Boundaries, the group behind Utah's 2018 independent redistricting initiative (Proposition 4). Elected in 2020. He sits on Ways and Means and was elected House Republican Conference Vice Chair on Nov. 8, 2023, and again Nov. 13, 2024 (Roll Call; Deseret News).

How he got here this year. Rep. Karianne Lisonbee won the Republican convention, 61.5% to 33.7% (Ballotpedia). Moore won the June 23 primary, 58.3% to 41.7% (Deseret News, AP count).

Who Crosby is. Lives in Cache Valley, where he has lived 19 years, with his wife and three daughters. Bachelor's and master's in political science from Utah State; an adjunct at USU's Center for Anticipatory Intelligence; earlier, operations and analytics work in a large company (his campaign site; UPR). He sent the Hive a statement, printed whole: In His Own Words.

Also on the ballot: Daniel Cottam (Libertarian), Carlton E. Bowen (Independent American) and Robert M. Moesinger (unaffiliated). Moesinger reports $8,805.75 raised and $5,401.76 in cash. Cottam's and Bowen's FEC pages show no money reports for 2025–26.

Dates. The Utah Debate Commission's 2nd District debate is Tuesday, Oct. 13, the day ballots mail (per Crosby's campaign releases; check utahdebatecommission.org).

THE GRADES

Graded Topic by Topic

IN PLAIN WORDSEach topic gets a grade, A (best) to F (worst), on five questions: Does it check power? Can the public see it? Is it clear who pays? What does it do to air, water and land? Do the people affected get a real say? N/A means the question doesn't fit. A dash means the record isn't documented enough to grade.

How to read these: grades judge only what is already in the record as of Oct. 2, 2026. They judge the record, not the person. A C means the record meets what the law requires, the status quo. A B or A means the candidate went beyond it, and the record shows it. Power on campaign money uses a fixed scale, the same for every candidate and party: A when 90% or more of the counted money comes from individual people, B at 67–89%, C at 34–66%, D at 10–33%, F under 10%. Party help and the candidate's own money are shown but not counted; own money is always stated. D means something is vague or bundled, or a public check is weakened; F means something required is missing, or a check is removed with nothing in its place. Candidates can amend a report at any time; when the record changes, the grade is updated and dated. Corrections are welcome at webercountyhive@gmail.com.

Overall is the average of the graded questions (A = 4, B = 3, C = 2, D = 1, F = 0; N/A and dashes are left out). A plus or minus shows where the average falls between letters, and an average exactly halfway counts toward the higher letter with a minus. Added Oct. 2, 2026.
