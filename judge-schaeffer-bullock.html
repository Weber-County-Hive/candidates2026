Hive judge-rubric grades

How to read the grades: A C means the record meets what Utah law requires, the status quo. A B or A means the judge went beyond it, and the record shows it. D means the judge passed JPEC’s minimum but JPEC rates that area below the judge’s peers; F means below JPEC’s legal minimum score of 3.6. Every grade is taken from JPEC’s own published wording, the same way for all 57 judges. No grade is based on how any case came out, or on who appointed the judge.

1. Fair to people in courtC
JPEC measure: Procedural Fairness. Passed every minimum standard JPEC applies to this kind of evaluation (4 of 4), including procedural fairness.
2. Follows the lawN/A
JPEC measure: Legal Ability. JPEC does not survey part-time judges; not counted.
3. Temperament & integrityN/A
JPEC measure: Integrity & Judicial Temperament. Not surveyed; not counted.
4. Runs the court wellN/A
JPEC measure: Administrative Skills. Not surveyed; not counted.
5. Public recordN/A
H.B. 540 (2026) requires judges to file annual financial disclosures (board seats, investments, real estate, a spouse’s job). The Judicial Council is writing the rules and no judge has filed one yet, so this is not graded for anyone. It will be graded with the same missing-item ladder used for candidates’ disclosure forms.
6. Retention moneyN/A
A judge may form one retention campaign committee and must report each contribution to the Lieutenant Governor within 31 days; an interim report is due seven days before the election (Oct. 27, 2026). No committee for this judge was found as of Oct. 17, 2026. That is not a finding that none exists; it will be checked again after Oct. 27.
OverallC
Average of the graded questions (A=4 … F=0; N/A left out): 2.0. A plus or minus shows where the average falls between letters.

How a grade can change: Questions 1–4 change only with JPEC’s next evaluation of this judge. Questions 5 and 6 will be graded once judges’ H.B. 540 disclosures are posted or a retention committee files reports.

JPEC’s scores

Why there are no scores here: JPEC does not run surveys for part-time justice court judges because too few attorneys appear before them for a reliable sample. Instead, trained courtroom observers watch the judge. The judge must pass every minimum standard (4 of 4).

What JPEC’s report says

The text below is JPEC’s own public report, reproduced as published. Quotes inside it are from JPEC’s survey respondents and courtroom observers, not from the Hive.

JPEC summary

Together, the three observers were enthusiastically positive about Judge Shaeffer-Bullock. This judge meets discipline standards set by statute and has been certified by the Judicial Council as meeting all time standards, education requirements, and mental and physical competence standards.

Courtroom observers, overall

  • Together, the three observers were enthusiastically positive about Judge Shaeffer-Bullock.
  • All observers reported that if they appeared before Judge Shaeffer-Bullock they would be treated fairly.

What observers agreed on

  • Observers variously reported that Judge Shaeffer-Bullock paid attention, listened to stories and arguments, and “seemed particularly attentive to…defendants.” She exhibited sincerity, kindness, and personability, and she “acknowledged and appreciated” defendants’ efforts, telling one of them to “stay sober and ‘Do good things with your life.’” She handled each case in “an even-handed manner” and showed consistency, and when “she saw the same type[s] of cases over and over again…the judge did not appear to lose interest and/or cut corners.” She appeared considerate of defendants’ needs, in one matter, working to restructure a defendant’s sentence who was experiencing “mental health challenges” so they could more easily comply. The judge operated in a thorough and exact fashion and “did not make anyone feel rushed.” She communicated clearly and was “super at explaining rights and procedures” to make sure defendants were informed.
  • All observers particularly emphasized the judge’s competencies. A couple of them described her as efficient, ensuring defendants listened initially to her instructions to minimize confusion and maximize time later, while a couple stated she was knowledgeable. One reported that she “was effective in creating breakout rooms and setting times for pretrial conferences,” while another found that “the judge knew more about the cases than the prosecutor.”

Other observations

  • Two observers spoke positively about the judge allowing participants to express themselves in court.

Biography (from JPEC)

Judge Kelly N. Schaeffer-Bullock was appointed to the Highland/Alpine City Justice Court in January 2017. Judge Schaeffer-Bullock graduated from Brigham Young University - Hawaii Campus in 2001 with a Bachelor of Arts, and later earned her Juris Doctor from the J. Reuben Clark Law School at Brigham Young University in 2008. Prior to appointment, Judge Schaeffer-Bullock had spent the majority of her legal career as a municipal prosecutor, though she gained civil experience when working for the law firm of Bugden & Isaacson, LLC. She also continues to serve as a lecturer and researcher at BYU's law school. Judge Schaeffer-Bullock is a member of the Daughters of the American Revolution, and devoted to the mission of that organization.
