Mike Bird vs. Luke Maynes

A Doers Network-endorsed Republican backed personally by the seat's own sitting Council Chair, running against a Democratic LGBTQ challenger — funded more by his own family than by any PAC.

What the records show

Bird has raised $31,305 through his June 15, 2026 report — and more than half of it, $17,000, came from three people who share his last name. The seat's sitting Council Chair, Aimee Winder Newton, personally gave him $6,000, reading less like a contested primary and more like a hand-off. Doers Network gave him $1,500 through its Utah filing, and two separate Doers Network Salt Lake County board members — Adam Gardiner and Seth Sunderland — each gave him money personally on top of that.

His race isn't on the state's disclosure system at all; Salt Lake County runs its own portal, a distinction worth knowing before concluding any county-level Doers-endorsed candidate has "no matching contribution."

01 The race

Mike Bird, a Director of Operations and Finance at University of Utah Health, announced his candidacy for Salt Lake County Council District 3 in January 2026 and won the seat's Republican convention nomination that April. He faces Democrat Luke Maynes in the November general election. District 3 covers Murray, Taylorsville, West Jordan, Midvale, and West Valley.

Bird previously ran for Utah House District 35 in 2024 and lost to Rosalba Dominguez.

The seat is currently held by Aimee Winder Newton, who chairs the County Council. Her own campaign contributions to Bird — detailed below — suggest she is stepping back and backing him as her successor rather than seeking re-election against him.

03 The succession question

Aimee Winder Newton, the sitting Chair of the Salt Lake County Council representing District 3, personally gave Bird $1,000 on January 6, 2026 and a second $5,000 on January 30, 2026 — $6,000 total, and his single largest institutional-adjacent donor after his own family. A sitting officeholder funding a candidate for her own seat, at this scale, reads as a backing-the-successor relationship rather than a contested race, though her own 2026 filing status hasn't been independently confirmed.

Worth confirming

Whether Winder Newton has filed for re-election to this seat, is term-limited, or is stepping back voluntarily hasn't been independently verified beyond the pattern her own contributions suggest.
