Case 04 · Utah House District 10 · East Ogden / South Ogden

The Nonprofit That Wasn't

Rosemary Lesser (D) vs. Jim Alvey (R) for the open seat Jill Koford is vacating to run for state Senate. A $35,000 same-day PAC infusion, a same-day-filed election calendar, and a nonprofit corporation Alvey dissolved the same day he was asked about it.

The race: Rosemary Lesser (D) vs. Jim Alvey (R, retired) — an open seat. Lesser represented HD10 from 2021–2024 but lost her re-election bid to Jill Koford (R) in 2024. Koford, the current officeholder, isn't running for re-election here — she's running for Senate District 5 instead, leaving HD10 open. District 10 covers East and South Ogden.

Same reporting-gap pattern already documented for Shelton and Sawyer

Alvey's entire 2026 report calendar — Convention, Primary, September 30th, and General — was pre-filed as empty shells well before each report's own period even began, then backfilled via amendment as real money arrived. His Primary Report was submitted 4/20/26, 52 days before that period even ended. His September 30th and General reports were both submitted 6/15/26 — the General Report covers 9/26–10/22/26, meaning it was "filed" over three months before its own period starts. A comparison check against NUPAC, H3 PAC, and Utah Forward PAC filings found none with a submission date predating their own report period — this pattern is not standard practice. Legally compliant (each amendment lands within the 31-day window) but it collapses public reporting checkpoints into one early "filed" status, so the $35,000 PAC infusion entered the record with no new filing event to signal it.

The address, and the negative balance

Alvey's very first 2026 filing, the Convention Report, already lists his campaign address as 6066 S 2400 E — the neighbor's parcel, not his own trust property — meaning this wasn't a late typo picked up along the way, it's been on file since the start. That same report shows he spent nearly $1,900 more than he'd raised at that point ($250.00 in, $2,148.69 out), including $450.00 paid to "Copper Nickel" — the event venue LLC Sheri Alvey founded in 2016 as its original organizer and sole member. Utah's corporate registry currently lists a different person, Craig Goodliffe, as the LLC's registered agent and manager — consistent with the Alveys' own bio claim that they sold the business in 2021, though the registry itself doesn't document that transaction. Current control isn't the Alveys, so this reads as a legitimate vendor payment rather than a self-dealing one.

The nonprofit corporation

Utah's corporate registry shows Alvey formed a nonprofit corporation, "VoteJimAlvey" (entity #14665172-0140), on 2/24/2026 — a separate legal entity from his CFIS-registered personal campaign committee of the same name. The Articles of Incorporation state a purpose of "organized exclusively for supporting a political candidate in the pursuit of public office," answer "No" to the charitable-organization questionnaire, specify no voting members, and list perpetual duration. Both "Jim Alvey" and "James Manley Alvey" signed as incorporators. A search of disclosures.utah.gov for "VOTEJIMALVEY" across all 2026 transaction types — contributions and expenditures — returns zero results.

8/17/2026, same day
Alvey dissolves the entity. Utah SOS record shows Status: Inactive, "Voluntarily Dissolved," updated 8/17/2026 — the same day the questions were raised. His follow-up comment: "There was 0 activity... It was unnecessary to create an EIN – a lesson I learned in the process," directly conceding the entity was unnecessary, at odds with his earlier claim a bank required it.
The contradictions in Alvey's own account: the entity's status was Active, not a pending bank application. The Articles contain a specific typed purpose clause naming political-candidate support, not a generic nonprofit checkbox. "No" was answered on the charitable-org questionnaire. No voting members and perpetual duration were both explicitly specified — unusual detail for something he describes as a formality. And the entity name is run together as one word with no spaces, which reads as intentional rather than accidental for something meant to be easily searchable.

Lesser's background

OB/GYN physician and U.S. Air Force veteran — part of the first class of women admitted to the University of Notre Dame, medical degree from the Uniformed Services University, 15 years in the Air Force serving military families in Germany and Texas. Practiced OB/GYN in Ogden from 1992–2020 at Ogden Women's Clinic, Ogden Regional Medical Center, and McKay-Dee Hospital. Appointed to HD10 in January 2021 to finish Rep. Lou Shurtliff's term after Shurtliff's death that December — Lesser learned of the vacancy on her last day of medical practice, was selected by Weber County Democratic delegates, and was appointed by Gov. Cox. Her husband Dave is also a physician; the two have volunteered providing surgical and emergency care in Mali and post-earthquake Haiti. Current board memberships: Boys & Girls Clubs of Weber-Davis, Envision Utah, Onstage Ogden, the Olene Walker Institute of Politics & Public Service, the Richards Institute for Ethics at Weber State University, the Women in Business Committee of the Ogden-Weber Chamber of Commerce, and Blue Star Families of Utah.

Community recognition: 2024 Ogden School Board Adult Education Champion Award; 2024 Community Leader Award, United Way of Northern Utah (for promoting the Connection Learning Campus and obtaining 211 Program funding); 2023 Community Champion, Utah Association for Infant Mental Health — for extending postpartum Medicaid benefits from 60 days to one year; 2023 award, Utah Public Employees' Association; "Outstanding Contribution by an Elected Official," Utah Association of Local Boards of Health, for her work on the House Health and Human Services Committee; Athena 2025, Ogden-Weber Chamber of Commerce; and the Salt Lake Chamber of Commerce's Legislative Business Champion Award four consecutive years.

Alvey's background

Born 1956 at the old Dee Hospital in Ogden; served an LDS mission in the Midlands of England at 19. Graduated Weber State College with a communications degree; was student graduation speaker for his class and served as Student Body Cultural Vice President. Career: physician liaison at McKay-Dee Hospital, then Student Services Coordinator at Weber State, then back to McKay-Dee/Intermountain Health as Assistant Director of Public Relations for 10 years and later Director of Public Relations and Marketing for Intermountain's Northern Region. He left to found Alvey Media Group, which his own campaign bio describes as continuing today after "almost 35 years," with Intermountain Health still a client alongside Gore-Tex, Browning, Springfield Armory, and Abbott Laboratories. Longtime Ogden Pioneer Days volunteer; he and wife Sheri restored the Browning Brothers buildings downtown and she founded the Copper Nickel Event Center, both sold in 2021, after which the couple served an LDS mission together in Nauvoo, Illinois.

A real discrepancy worth noting: Alvey's financial disclosure (conflict-of-interest form) lists him and spouse Sheri as "Retired," with no current business entities and no $5,000+ income sources. His own campaign bio, published the same cycle, describes Alvey Media Group as an active, nearly-35-year-old company with major ongoing national clients. Utah's corporate registry separately shows he was president/director/agent of a different entity, "Jim Alvey Productions Inc.," inactive since 2021 — but that's not the same company his bio describes as still operating. Weber County property records show his trust owns 6062 S 2400 East, Ogden (2026 market value $662,777) — his campaign's own registered address, 6066 S 2400 East, actually belongs to a different owner, the John Michael Stevens Revocable Trust.

His campaign platform names seven priorities: reducing financial pressures on families (taxes, "attainable housing," wise spending), public safety, education (parent-driven options, explicit support for Weber State), conservative values (self-reliance, voter ID), a pro-life and biological-sex position, "Keeping Government Accountable and Transparent to You," and managing growth. His site's own commencement-speech anecdote states he shared the stage with Elliot Richardson, described as "United States Attorney General under President Gerald Ford" — Richardson was actually Attorney General under Nixon; his later Cabinet post under Ford was Secretary of Commerce.
