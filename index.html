Voter Quick Scan

R or D is printed on the ballot. Who pays for the campaign isn't. Tap any card to see where the money came from and what the record shows.

Vote them outIncumbents only. Any one of: their own words contradicted by something they controlled (a vote, a bill they filed, a disclosure form). A bill failing to pass never counts, since that depends on the other 103 legislators · 20% or more of their money from the insider network (leadership and caucus PACs; network PACs such as Doers, CMV, All In For Utah, Lincoln Hill, Utah Forward, Conservative Millennials, UINPAC and SenDemPAC; and sitting officeholders giving $1,000 or more). Doesn't apply when gifts from individual donors outweigh all PAC, business, party and insider money combined. A candidate's own money never counts against them, but it doesn't cancel out PAC money either · their campaign gives to other officeholders.
Looks like a skunkNot in the seat yet, but already trips the same rules: said vs. did, 20%+ insider money, or gives like an insider. Looks like a skunk, acts like a skunk, stinks like a skunk.
C-R = Corporate-funded RepublicanR = Republican · D = Democrat · FWD = Forward Party · U = Unaffiliated · WI = Write-in. A C is added in front of the party letter (C-R, C-D) when 30% or more of reported campaign money came from corporations and industry groups. In most Utah districts the Republican nomination decides the seat, so the R on the ballot can say more about where a candidate can win than about who they answer to. The C shows who is paying. Unions, party committees, leadership PACs and officeholders' checks are counted separately and never make a C. Insider money can bring a callout, but only corporate and industry money can bring a C.
