House District 12 · General Election, Nov. 3, 2026

Anna Graff vs. Mike Schultz

A grassroots challenger running on individual, small-dollar support against the sitting Speaker of the Utah House, whose personal account and three-PAC network hold more than $700,000 combined. Roy and Hooper, Weber County.

Updated Sept. 30, 2026, with Schultz's September 30th Report and Graff's contributions through Sept. 16.

Incumbent: Mike Schultz (R), Speaker of the House, since 2015 Challenger: Anna Graff (D) Primary: Uncontested
What the records show

Graff's campaign has raised about $4,341 in 2026, based on contributions filed through Sept. 16. About two-thirds came from individual donors, most in gifts of $5 to $100; the rest came from Democratic party groups, in-kind support and $157 of her own money. No PAC or corporate money.

Schultz's personal campaign account has raised $73,100 in 2026. $35,000 of it (48%) is his own money, given on Aug. 13; $36,100 (49%) came from companies, PACs and associations. It held $89,282.60 on Sept. 25.

Through their August reports, Schultz's personal campaign account and the three PACs he controls or has decision-making authority over — House Speaker Leadership PAC, Utah House Republican Election Committee, and Northern Wasatch HBA PAC — hold a combined ~$712,000 in cash on hand. The PACs are funded by corporate PACs, industry associations and major individual donors giving in the thousands; his personal account is now about half his own money.

These figures update our July 2, 2026 piece, "Two Funding Frameworks," which reported $989.68 for Graff and $335,150 combined for Schultz's ecosystem at that point in the cycle. Updated Sept. 30, 2026: Schultz's personal account uses his September 30th Report; Graff's figures use her contributions filed through Sept. 16; the three PACs still use their August filings.

Beyond what Schultz's PACs raise, the record shows where it flows once it arrives — a consulting fee here, a network candidate there, and one unresolved, unusual line item.

Palmer's firm and PAC network already appears across multiple other Weber County candidate files this cycle.

Election Hive — $283.36

UHREC paid $283.36 to Election Hive, a statewide political consulting and money-processing firm run by Matthew K. Lusty, coded "Advertising." Election Hive is not Schultz's own entity — it serves dozens of clients from Gov. Cox (its largest documented client, with six-figure single-day payments) down to county-level candidates. The firm filed zero annual reports between October 2019 and June 2024 while an estimated $270,000+ moved through it in 2023 alone and $1.54 million+ in 2024.

Cross-reference (unpublished — no case file or article yet)
PAC Management Services (VIPGateKeeper) — $5,000

Schultz's personal campaign account received $5,000 on 3/10/2026 from "PAC Management Services," Fairfax, Virginia. The firm does business as VIPGateKeeper — a real-time donation platform where wealthy "VIP" donors pre-fund an account and pre-approved "Gatekeeper" advisors push contributions to candidates on demand. That means the true source of the $5,000 is an unnamed VIP donor whose identity never has to appear on Utah's public disclosure. The same entity gave Rep. Jason Thompson (HD 3) $250 sixteen days later, on 3/26/2026 — a national search of state and federal campaign-finance databases turned up no other public record of this platform giving to any candidate anywhere else. Schultz and Thompson appear to be its only two documented recipients in the country.

03 Mike Schultz

Schultz has represented HD 12 since 2015 and has served as Speaker of the Utah House since November 2023, succeeding Brad Wilson. He's a real estate developer and president of Castle Creek Homes, a lifelong resident of Hooper, Roy, and West Haven.

Separately from this campaign-finance ecosystem, Schultz personally donated $120,000 to Preserving Utah Values PAC on Nov. 30, 2025 — reported as the PAC's sole financial backer. That PAC ran anti-transgender attack ads against more than a dozen Democratic candidates in the 2024 cycle, including messaging that helped target Rosemary Lesser, who lost her HD10 seat by 309 votes.

Legislative record

Schultz chief-sponsored three numbered bills in the 2026 session. Sentencing Amendments (HB 274, floor sponsor Sen. Michael K. McKell) and the Statewide Catalyst Campus Model (HB 447, floor sponsor Sen. Ann Millner) both passed and were signed into law. Ballot Title Amendments (HB 563, floor sponsor Senate President J. Stuart Adams) remains filed in the House and has not passed. A fourth bill, Occupational Licensing Revisions, was abandoned before it received a number. He also cosponsored several bills that passed, including the State Sovereignty Fund (HB 464) and Drug Trafficking Amendments (HB 087), and cosponsored two that did not pass this session: a resolution on transitioning people from government benefits to self-reliance (HCR 8) and a bill banning foreign funding in ballot propositions and elections (HB 393).

HB 274's floor sponsor, Sen. McKell, also carried HB 392 and HB 366 as Senate sponsor — the bills that created Utah's new three-judge constitutional panels as part of the broader 2026 judicial-reform package (SB 134, HB 392, HB 366, SB 233, HB 540). When the Utah State Bar warned HB 366 could "threaten judicial stability," Schultz told KUER he was "confident in our constitutional authority to do what we've done."

Schultz sits on the Business and Labor Interim Committee, Executive Appropriations Committee, House Business, Labor, and Commerce Committee, House Natural Resources, Agriculture, and Environment Committee, and the Natural Resources, Agriculture, and Environment Interim Committee, and chairs the Legislative Management Committee.

Platform vs. record

Schultz's campaign site says government should create the conditions for people and businesses to thrive "and then get out of the way," crediting himself with passing the largest tax cut in Utah's history and pledging to keep fighting government regulation and "unnecessary government red tape." His site's Federalism page frames him as a defender of the Constitution against "corrupt special interest groups," pledging his loyalty is to the Constitution and to voters.

Worth reading side by side

The bill titles line up closely with his own campaign language: he cosponsored the "State Sovereignty Fund" (signed into law) and a resolution on transitioning residents "from government benefits to self-reliance" — both echoing his site's federalism and self-reliance messaging almost word for word.

At the same time, his own record includes expanding rather than shrinking state judicial and prosecutorial infrastructure — HB 274 adds seats to the Sentencing Commission, and the broader judicial-reform stack he publicly defended expands the Supreme Court and Court of Appeals while giving the governor more appointment control.

His messaging about guarding against "special interest groups" sits alongside a campaign-finance ecosystem funded almost entirely by corporate PACs, industry associations, and major individual donors giving $5,000 to $15,000 at a time — Utah Bankers Association, Larry H Miller Company, HOALiving Holdings LLC, and PacifiCorp among them.

04 Anna Graff

Graff describes on her campaign site becoming a single parent roughly a decade ago after leaving an abusive marriage, and says navigating housing instability and her family's needs during that period shaped her focus on accessible community resources. She later married her husband, an active-duty Air Force officer, built a career in the arts, and became involved in community advocacy before joining her city's Planning Commission.

Graff serves on her city's Planning Commission and describes speaking on bills during past Utah Legislative Sessions as a resident. This is her first run for state office; there is no legislative voting record to compare against her platform.

Her campaign site names four priorities: Preserving the State, Housing & Affordability, Promoting Accessibility, and Oversight & Accountability. On accessibility, she argues that designing for people facing the highest barriers improves the experience for everyone, and that the issue extends beyond ADA-required physical access to website design, access to public records, transportation, and medical care — noting that many areas of life remain exempt or grandfathered in, and that upgrade costs can put compliance out of reach even for a small business that wants to make them. On oversight, she points to public-funds programs and the checks and balances across the judicial, legislative, and executive branches, saying she wants to "strengthen our avenues to ensure accountability, not protections from responsibility." She frames her campaign around amplifying the perspectives of groups including people with disabilities, single parents, military families, minorities, retirees, young families, students, and unhoused residents.

Graff is running her campaign on individual and small-dollar support, with contributions ranging from $1 to $1,000 and several recurring monthly donors giving $5-$10 at a time.
