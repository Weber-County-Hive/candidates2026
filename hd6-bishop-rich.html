Eight Things the Records Show

IN PLAIN WORDSA long-time Washington lawmaker is back in the Utah House. Much of his money comes from lobbying firms, trade groups and companies, and a few dollars come from the towns he represents. His opponent runs almost entirely on help from his party and his own company.

  1. Bishop's money comes mostly from groups, and from outside the district. 22 businesses, PACs and associations gave $18,251 (56.7%). 16 individuals gave $11,930 (37.1%). He lent or gave $2,000 himself (6.2%). Only one gift, $100 from Brigham City, lists a Box Elder or Weber County address other than his own.
  2. A lobbying firm's check, and the same owner's campaign vendor. Stokes Strategies gave $1,000 on May 5. Bishop's largest vendor, Roost Co. ($3,303.15), is Roost Communications LLC, whose state-listed members are Spencer Stokes and Matthew J. Holton, at the same South Temple address as Stokes Strategies.
  3. Three Doers Network board members gave $2,100. Adam Gardiner ($1,000), Cody Stewart ($1,000) and Steve Hiatt ($100) sit on the Doers Network's county boards. The Doers Network itself does not appear as a Bishop donor in the state's 2026 records.
  4. $6,000 arrived the day before he was sworn in. On May 5, seven checks came from UCU PAC, Stokes Strategies, the Utah Association of Realtors, Comcast, Lincoln Hill Partners PAC, MJH Advisors and one individual. He took the oath May 6.
  5. One gift is reported differently by the giver. Bishop's report lists $1,000 from the Utah Association of Realtors. The Realtors' own Primary Report, amended June 16, lists a $1,000 payment to "Bishop for House" as deleted.
  6. A board seat on his form, and one that isn't. His form lists one board, Cambridge Prep Academy, a Provo charter school. The website of Utah Citizens, a group that organizes residents to contact legislators, presents him on its home page under "Our Leadership" and lists him on its "Board of Directors" page, but the group's state registry filing lists four directors and he is not one of them.
  7. Rich's campaign runs on party help. The Forward Party of Utah gave $2,554.42 in voter-data analysis and $500 in video production. His software company gave $550. Individuals gave $1,286, about $185 of it from Perry.
  8. Bishop has no state House votes yet. He was sworn in after the 2026 session ended, and no special session has been held since. His congressional record on public lands is graded below.

How Bishop got the seat. Rep. Matthew Gwynn resigned in March, citing family reasons (KSL). Republican delegates picked Bishop at the state convention on April 25 (KSL). He was sworn in May 6 and serves through January 2027 (KUTV). He is also the Republican nominee for the full term.

Who he is. He taught high school history, government, debate and German at Box Elder and Ben Lomond high schools from 1974 to 2002, served in the Utah House from 1979 to 1994 (Speaker in his last two years), chaired the Utah Republican Party from 1997 to 2001, and represented Utah's 1st Congressional District from 2003 to 2021, where he chaired the House Natural Resources Committee (his bio on utahconstitution.com; KUTV). His disclosure form lists him as retired, most recently an adjunct teacher at Utah State University.

Who Rich is. James David Rich of Perry is president and a full-time software developer at Edward A.E. Rich and Associates, a Salt Lake City business-accounting software company (his disclosure form). BallotReady lists a University of Utah bachelor's degree (2002) and his presidency of the company since 2008. He is the Forward Party nominee. No Democrat filed.

In his own bio. His campaign site says he started at the company in an entry-level job, learned business software programming from his father, and bought the company in 2008. He studied jazz composition at the University of Utah, along with math and physics, and was student body vice president at Olympus High School in Salt Lake City. He calls Perry his hometown and says he volunteers as a morning crossing guard at Promontory School (move.forwardwith.us, About and People and Places pages, read Oct. 2, 2026).

Who these firms are

Roost Co. is Roost Communications LLC (state entity 13310231-0160, formed March 15, 2023). State business records list its members as Spencer Stokes and Matthew J. Holton, with Stokes as registered agent, at 505 East South Temple, Suite 200. Stokes Strategies, Stokes's lobbying firm, gives from the same address; Bishop's report lists that address for its $1,000. Roost also appears as a vendor in the Hive's HD-10, HD-17 and SD-5 files.

MJH Advisors ($250) is the firm of registered lobbyist Matt Holton, per the Hive's earlier lobbyist research. State records list Roost member Matthew J. Holton at 7430 South Parkridge Circle; Bishop's report lists MJH Advisors at 7340 South Parkridge Circle. If these are the same Holton, both of Roost's owners' lobbying firms gave on May 5, and Roost was Bishop's largest vendor.

AC Strategies received $2,553.05 on July 21. Bishop's report gives no address for it. Utah's Division of Corporations lists one Utah company by that name: AC Strategies, LLC (entity 12115697-0160), formed Jan. 14, 2021, with one member, Richard Austin Cox of Midvale. The state administratively dissolved it on June 26, 2024 after it missed its renewal; it shows as inactive. Bishop's payment is dated July 21, 2026, two years later. His report gives no address for the payee, so the record doesn't show whether this is the same business, a business using the name another way, or a company registered elsewhere (active companies named AC Strategies exist in Delaware, California, Maryland, Tennessee, Ohio, Texas and Alabama). Education First and the Utah Association of Realtors also reported 2026 payments to "AC Strategies" (below). Education First, which gave Bishop $1,000, paid AC Strategies $1,355 for signature gathering on May 1, 2026, per the Hive's Education First file. The Utah Association of Realtors' own Primary Report shows three payments to "AC Strategies LLC" ($20,000, $3,000 and $2,500, the last for "Signature Gathering"), all marked deleted in its June 16 amendment.

Two filings, two answers. Bishop's report lists $1,000 from the Utah Association of Realtors, dated May 5, marked as amended. The Realtors' own Primary Report, amended June 16, lists a $1,000 payment to "Bishop for House" dated May 6 among 433 entries it deleted. Both amounts are shown here; the filings don't explain the difference.

The board seat on his form: Cambridge Prep

Cambridge Preparatory Academy is a K-8 charter school at 1724 South State Street in Provo, capped at 450 students, authorized by the State Charter School Board. Its charter application, posted on the state's Public Notice Website, lists "Robert Bishop (Director)" with the bio "Educator-Semi-retired." The board chair listed is Stan Lockhart. On Feb. 12, 2026, the State Charter School Board voted to let the school open in fall 2026 instead of 2027-28. The Hive did not confirm that it opened.

Cambridge Preparatory Academy, Inc. is a Utah nonprofit corporation (entity 14592591-0140) formed July 21, 2025; Stan Lockhart is its registered agent and incorporator. March 2026 State Board of Education minutes record the school withdrawing a waiver request.

Where it meets his donors: SEL Inc of Pleasant Grove gave Bishop $250 on Sept. 8. The Hive's earlier research, from a copy of state business records, lists Stan Lockhart as SEL Inc's registered agent. Education First Utah, an education-policy group, gave $1,000. As a legislator he votes on charter-school funding and rules.

A board page that isn't on his form: Utah Citizens

The website utahconstitution.com, signed "Utah Citizens," organizes "Impact Councils" of residents who text and email their legislators weekly using talking points the group provides. Its home page quotes Bishop under "Our Leadership": "Federalism is the government system that provides the solution to our problems and the salvation of our nation." Its page headed "Board of Directors" lists him with eight others, including former Rep. Phil Lyman and Jen Brown.

The state form asks for every entity in which the filer serves "in a paid or unpaid position on a board of directors." His form lists only Cambridge Prep. The site gives no legal name, nonprofit status or address. Utah's Division of Corporations lists Utah Citizens for the Constitution, a nonprofit corporation (entity 12373457-0140) formed June 30, 2021, active and last renewed June 30, 2026. Jennifer Brown, who is on the site's board list, is its registered agent and one of its directors. The site itself never uses the full name, so the match between the website and the registered corporation rests on the name and on Jennifer Brown appearing on both. The registry lists four directors, last updated in September 2024: Brooke Johnson, Douglas Brunnette, Kent Nelson and Jennifer Brown. Bishop is not among the directors in the state registry. So the group's website and its state filing list different boards: the website names nine people, including Bishop, while the registry names four, three of whom appear on the website only as profile pages. On this record, his form's answer matches the state registry; the website does not.

BISHOP'S RECORD

No State Votes Yet; a Long Federal Record

IN PLAIN WORDSBishop joined after this year's session, so he hasn't cast a vote in the Utah House this term. He serves on the Legislature's Federalism Commission. In Congress, his best-known work was on federal land, and that record is graded below.

Utah House, 2026

Sworn in May 6, 2026, after the 2026 General Session ended March 6. The Legislature's website lists no special session since then, so there are no floor votes to grade. Grade: PENDING.

Committee. The Legislature's 2026 interim roster for the Federalism Commission lists Rep. Rob Bishop as a voting member. Its nonvoting members include former congressmen Jason Chaffetz, Chris Stewart and Ben McAdams. Before he returned to the Legislature, an Aug. 18, 2025 commission agenda listed him presenting on federal involvement in education.

Not on the record: no public statement by Bishop on the proposed Stratos data center in Box Elder County's Hansel Valley was found in KSL, Utah News Dispatch, the Salt Lake Tribune, KUER or UPR coverage.

BEFORE 2026

Forty-Seven Years in Utah Politics

IN PLAIN WORDSBishop isn't new. He served in the Utah House, chaired the state Republican Party, spent 18 years in Congress and ran for lieutenant governor before returning to the Legislature this year. This section lays out that record and where his old federal campaign money went. Facts are marked with their source. Anything we could not confirm is listed separately at the end.

1979–1994

Utah House of Representatives; Speaker in the early 1990s. Sources disagree on the exact years. The House Historian lists 1979–1994 and Speaker 1992–1994; LegiStorm lists Speaker 1991–1993. The Hive uses the House Historian's dates.

1997–2001

Utah Republican Party chair, May 1997 to August 2001 (Wikipedia, secondary).

2003–2021

U.S. House, 1st District. He chaired the House Natural Resources Committee and later served as its ranking Republican. The House Historian lists his occupations as "teacher; professional advocate."

2012

Attended the signing of Utah's Transfer of Public Lands Act (H.B. 148), which demanded the federal government turn over federal land to the state.

2017

Advocated the Bears Ears and Grand Staircase-Escalante reductions. His Antiquities Act rewrite, H.R. 3990, cleared his committee 23–17 (graded above).

Jan. 2020

Named running mate for Thomas Wright's campaign for governor. The ticket finished behind Spencer Cox in the June primary (KSL).

Dec. 2020

Had a mild stroke shortly before leaving Congress (Salt Lake Tribune, Dec. 9, 2020).

2021

Appointed by Speaker Brad Wilson to the Utah Independent Redistricting Commission; resigned Oct. 25, 2021 (KSL).

2021–2022

His federal committee gave away its last money, including $2,000 to Utah Citizens for the Constitution, and closed July 6, 2022.

2023–2025

Spoke at UVU's Herbert Institute (Nov. 2023), appeared on the PoliticIt podcast (Oct. 2024), and presented to the Legislature's Federalism Commission (Aug. 2025).

2026

Won the House District 6 special election by delegate vote April 25 and was sworn in May 6.

THE GRADES

Graded Topic by Topic

IN PLAIN WORDSEach topic gets a grade, A (best) to F (worst), on five questions: Does it check power? Can the public see it? Is it clear who pays? What does it do to air, water and land? Do the people affected get a real say? N/A means the question doesn't fit. PENDING means it hasn't happened yet.

How to read these: grades judge only what is already in the record as of Oct. 2, 2026. They judge the record, not the person. C means the record meets what Utah law requires, the status quo. B or A means it goes beyond that in a way the record shows. D means something is vague or bundled, or a public check is weakened. F means something required is missing, or a check is removed with nothing in its place. Candidates can amend a report or form at any time; when the record changes, the grade is updated and dated. Corrections are welcome at webercountyhive@gmail.com.

Overall is the average of the graded questions (A = 4, B = 3, C = 2, D = 1, F = 0; N/A and dashes are left out). A plus or minus shows where the average falls between letters, and an average exactly halfway counts toward the higher letter with a minus. Added Oct. 2, 2026.
