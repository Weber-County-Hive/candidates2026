Hive judge-rubric grades

How to read the grades: A C means the record meets what Utah law requires, the status quo. A B or A means the judge went beyond it, and the record shows it. D means the judge passed JPEC’s minimum but JPEC rates that area below the judge’s peers; F means below JPEC’s legal minimum score of 3.6. Every grade is taken from JPEC’s own published wording, the same way for all 57 judges. No grade is based on how any case came out, or on who appointed the judge.

H.B. 540 (2026) requires judges to file annual financial disclosures (board seats, investments, real estate, a spouse’s job). The Judicial Council is writing the rules and no judge has filed one yet, so this is not graded for anyone. It will be graded with the same missing-item ladder used for candidates’ disclosure forms.
6. Retention moneyN/A
A judge may form one retention campaign committee and must report each contribution to the Lieutenant Governor within 31 days; an interim report is due seven days before the election (Oct. 27, 2026). No committee for this judge was found as of Oct. 17, 2026. That is not a finding that none exists; it will be checked again after Oct. 27.

How a grade can change: Questions 1–4 change only with JPEC’s next evaluation of this judge. Questions 5 and 6 will be graded once judges’ H.B. 540 disclosures are posted or a retention committee files reports.

What JPEC’s report says

The text below is JPEC’s own public report, reproduced as published. Quotes inside it are from JPEC’s survey respondents and courtroom observers, not from the Hive.

JPEC summary

Survey Respondent Recommendation: 98% of survey respondents recommend Judge Chin for retention.

Performance Measures: Survey scores are analyzed for statistical significance to determine if the judge scored well above, above, consistent with, below, or well below their justice court peers. Above Peers: Legal Ability, Integrity & Judicial Temperament, Administrative Skills, Procedural Fairness

Respondent and Observer Feedback:Respondents praise Judge Chin as particularly skilled, highlighting his legal acumen and diligent, orderly manner. One called him “the epitome of fairness.”

Both respondents and courtroom observers applaud his respectful, cordial demeanor, finding him to be caring but also firm, and both find that he communicates clearly.

All four observers report confidence that they would be treated fairly if they were to appear in Judge Chin’s court.

Courtroom observers, overall

  • Together, the four observers were strongly positive about Judge Chin.
  • All observers reported that if they appeared before Judge Chin they would be treated fairly.

What observers agreed on

  • Observers variously reported that Judge Chin projected as unbiased and neutral and balanced “firmness and compassion” in holding participants accountable. He acknowledged their circumstances and was open to giving second chances, “reinforcing the seriousness of the situation while offering a path forward.” He demonstrated “a thoughtful and methodical approach to each case,” and, in one matter, when a minor presented without parents, he “confirmed that the individual’s parents were aware of the court appearance.” He gave all participants “the opportunity to express their positions and ask questions.” He was clear and “well-spoken” in explanations so that “everyone in his courtroom understood the proceedings.”
  • All observers particularly emphasized the Judge’s respectful demeanor and treatment of participants. They variously described him as calm, patient, and polite but stern. According to one, he said “please” and “thank you” frequently and “greeted participants warmly,” while another found that he “showed respect to all” and was “cordial at the end of each proceeding.” A third observed that the judge “cared…about each defendant and hoped they would do better.”

Other observations

  • Two observers commented favorably regarding the judge’s preparedness and efficiency as well as respect for others’ time.
    • One described the judge as “effective,” while the second said that he “was able to keep proceedings moving [as] efficiently as possible when juggling multiple arraignments and hearings.” In addition, both found him to be respectful of people’s time by apologizing to parties that had to wait.

Biography (from JPEC)

Judge Augustus G. Chin was appointed to the Holladay Justice Court in 2011. The Holladay Justice Court serves the cities of Holladay, Cottonwood Heights, Millcreek, and Brighton Township. Judge Chin received his Juris Doctorate from the University of Utah College of Law. He also has a Bachelor of Arts degree in Political Science and a minor in Spanish from the University of Utah. Judge Chin was a law clerk-bailiff and law clerk for 3rd District Judge Tyrone E. Medley from 1995-1997. He was a prosecutor for the Salt Lake City Prosecutor’s Office and the Summit County Attorney’s Office from 1998-2008. Prior to taking the bench in 2011, he worked as a criminal defense attorney for Wasatch Advocates and Rasmussen and Miner. Service history: National Judicial College, Part-time (volunteer) Faculty 2017-present, Judicial Council 2017-2023, Board of Justice Court Judges 2016-2023, Utah State Bar President 2006–2007, Utah State Bar Commissioner 1999–2008, Utah Minority Bar Association President 1998-1999, Law-Related Education 1998-2025, Board Member 2009-2025, Chair 2011–2013 & 2023-2024. Utah Bar Foundation 2008-2011, Utah Supreme Court Advisory Committee on Professionalism and Civility 2002-2015.
