Eight Things the Records Show

IN PLAIN WORDSShelley's 2026 campaign money came from House Republican leadership PACs, a few industry groups and his own reimbursements; no other person gave. Mashek's small campaign runs mostly on gifts from people. Hatch has no money on file. Shelley's carbon-credit bill, as introduced and as passed, left one kind of carbon dioxide use out of the definition of a carbon credit; his own form lists a company that supplies industrial gas.

  1. $4,500 of Shelley's $10,783 came from two House leadership PACs. The Utah House Republican Election Committee (UHREC) gave $3,500 and the House Speaker Leadership PAC gave $1,000, together 41.7% of everything his campaign took in this year.
  2. No individual donor other than Shelley appears on his 2026 reports. The rest came from U-CAR PAC ($751), the Utah Petroleum Association PAC ($200), the law firm Parsons Behle & Latimer ($200), $4,783.03 from Shelley himself and $349.35 from the State of Utah.
  3. Shelley's own money is listed as reimbursements. Each of his three 2026 entries is described as reimbursing the account for a session motel or hotel stay, after the campaign paid Kimball Apartments $4,029 for "Travel Expenses" in January and February. The State of Utah's $349.35 is described as reimbursing a $349.35 Deer Valley Hyatt payment.
  4. His campaign gave $1,000 to Friends of Karianne Lisonbee on May 11, 2026. The entry was first filed as a contribution with the note "Donate to Congressional Campaign," then deleted and re-entered as a payment the same day.
  5. In 2024 a company on his form loaned his campaign $12,000. Sanpete Stone, which his form lists with Air Free Wireless Inc., made a "Loan from Company funds" on Dec. 3, 2024. The campaign paid Sanpete Stone back $10,278.31 and paid Shelley $4,000 the same day the loan came in.
  6. H.B. 185, his carbon-credit bill, excluded "an industrial or commercial use of liquefied carbon dioxide" from what counts as a carbon credit. The exclusion was in the bill as introduced (alongside a 25% tax on carbon credit sales) and stayed in the law that passed. His form lists Savoy Energy LLC, described as supplying "industrial gas to a pipeline for purification and processing." Side-by-side facts, set out below; the records don't say why the line was written.
  7. Corrected Oct. 20: Twist Gulch, LLC is not treated as missing from Shelley's form. OpenCorporates' copy of the Utah registry had listed him as its governing person. The state registry's own page, read Oct. 20, 2026, lists Brayden Shelley of Manti as governing person and registered agent, updated Aug. 24, 2026. Every company the registry lists under Troy Shelley's name is on his form.
  8. Mashek's and Hatch's forms list what the registry shows. Mashek lists her store, the Fayette Merc, and her town council seat; Hatch lists The Relief Mine Company. A registry search found no other active businesses for either.

H.B. 185 (2026), CARBON CREDIT AMENDMENTS · PRIMARY SPONSOR TROY SHELLEY

A 25% Tax That Was Dropped, and a CO2 Line That Stayed

IN PLAIN WORDSShelley's bill started as a 25% tax on selling carbon credits. By the time it passed, the tax was gone and the bill instead set up a fund for the attorney general to fight federal carbon rules. One line stayed the whole way: payments tied to selling carbon dioxide for industrial or commercial use are not "carbon credits" under Utah law. Shelley's form lists a company that supplies industrial gas.

THE BILL'S TEXT
SHELLEY'S OWN FILINGS
Introduced H.B. 185, Section 10, 79-6-1301(1)(b): "'Carbon credit' does not include a payment related to: (i) a right or interest associated with a regulated pollutant, as that term is defined in Title V of the 1990 Clean Air Act; or (ii) an industrial or commercial use of liquefied carbon dioxide." The same exclusion is in the version that became law.
His form lists Savoy Energy LLC and Savoy Wells LLC, described as supplying "industrial gas to a pipeline for purification and processing," and "Savoy Energy Well #1" as a $5,000+ income source. The state registry lists him as manager of Savoy CO2 Well # 1, LLC and of Savoy Energy, LLC.
Introduced 59-35-301: a 25% tax on "a sale of carbon credit," with a license required to sell one (59-35-201). Dropped in substitute S04.
With the exclusion, a payment tied to an industrial or commercial use of liquefied carbon dioxide would not have been a carbon credit, so the 25% tax as introduced would not have reached it.
Final law: the attorney general may use the Carbon Credit Litigation Fund to prevent federal cap-and-trade, mandatory carbon emissions reporting, greenhouse-gas claims against the state and climate remediation programs. State entities must report carbon credit sales to the State Auditor, who reports yearly to the Natural Resources, Agriculture, and Environmental Quality Appropriations Subcommittee.
Shelley sits on that subcommittee, the House Public Utilities and Energy Committee and the Public Utilities, Energy, and Technology Interim Committee (le.utah.gov profile). In December 2025 the Wyoming Energy Authority paid $1,262.43 for a "Tour of Carbon Injection facility/Coal mine" (his 2025 report).

Votes: House committee favorable 8-3 (Feb. 27), substitute adopted 11-0; House passed 70-0 (March 2); Senate committee 4-0; Senate passed 19-6 (March 4); signed March 25, 2026. Sen. Derrin Owens was the Senate sponsor; Owens's campaign gave Shelley $1,000 in 2024.

What this does and doesn't show. The bill text and Shelley's filings are both public records, set side by side. Utah's form asks legislators to list their businesses, and Shelley's does. Nothing in the records read says why the liquefied carbon dioxide line was written or who asked for it, whether Shelley's companies sell liquefied carbon dioxide, or whether he declared a conflict during debate. Legislators may vote on bills that touch their own industries; Utah's rules ask them to disclose it.

THE GRADES

Graded Topic by Topic

IN PLAIN WORDSEach topic gets a grade, A (best) to F (worst), on five questions: Does it check power? Can the public see it? Is it clear who pays? What does it do to air, water and land? Do the people affected get a real say? N/A means the question doesn't fit. A dash means the record isn't documented enough to grade. PENDING means a record the grade depends on hasn't been read yet.

How to read these: grades judge only what is already in the record as of Oct. 13, 2026. They judge the record, not the person. A C means the record meets what Utah law requires, the status quo. A B or A means the candidate went beyond it, and the record shows it. Power on campaign money uses a fixed scale, the same for every candidate and party: A when 90% or more of the counted money comes from individual people, B at 67–89%, C at 34–66%, D at 10–33%, F under 10%. Party help and the candidate's own money are shown but not counted; own money is always stated. D means something is vague or bundled, or a public check is weakened; F means something required is missing, or a check is removed with nothing in its place. Candidates can amend a report or form at any time; when the record changes, the grade is updated and dated. Corrections are welcome at webercountyhive@gmail.com.

Overall is the average of the graded questions (A = 4, B = 3, C = 2, D = 1, F = 0; N/A, dashes and PENDING are left out). A plus or minus shows where the average falls between letters.
