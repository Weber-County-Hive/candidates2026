Hive judge-rubric grades

How to read the grades: A C means the record meets what Utah law requires, the status quo. A B or A means the judge went beyond it, and the record shows it. D means the judge passed JPEC’s minimum but JPEC rates that area below the judge’s peers; F means below JPEC’s legal minimum score of 3.6. Every grade is taken from JPEC’s own published wording, the same way for all 57 judges. No grade is based on how any case came out, or on who appointed the judge.

5. Public recordN/A
H.B. 540 (2026) requires judges to file annual financial disclosures (board seats, investments, real estate, a spouse’s job). The Judicial Council is writing the rules and no judge has filed one yet, so this is not graded for anyone. It will be graded with the same missing-item ladder used for candidates’ disclosure forms.
6. Retention moneyN/A
A judge may form one retention campaign committee and must report each contribution to the Lieutenant Governor within 31 days; an interim report is due seven days before the election (Oct. 27, 2026). No committee for this judge was found as of Oct. 17, 2026. That is not a finding that none exists; it will be checked again after Oct. 27.

How a grade can change: Questions 1–4 change only with JPEC’s next evaluation of this judge. Questions 5 and 6 will be graded once judges’ H.B. 540 disclosures are posted or a retention committee files reports.

JPEC’s scores

Why there are no scores here: JPEC does not run surveys for part-time justice court judges because too few attorneys appear before them for a reliable sample. Instead, trained courtroom observers watch the judge and JPEC staff interview people leaving court. The judge must pass every minimum standard (5 of 5).

What JPEC’s report says

The text below is JPEC’s own public report, reproduced as published. Quotes inside it are from JPEC’s survey respondents and courtroom observers, not from the Hive.

JPEC summary

Appointed in 2014, Judge McIff received positive reviews from the observer and all respondents. The judge presented as a methodical, calm judge who made sure that participants understood their rights and responsibilities in court. He allowed them to express themselves and was attentive to their needs. This judge meets discipline standards set by statute and has been certified by the Judicial Council as meeting all time standards, education requirements, and mental and physical competence standards.

How JPEC gathered this report

Judge McIff’s report consists of a two-hour observation conducted on three different days via WebEx videoconferencing. It also consists of 14 interviews conducted in-person. The interview sample includes litigants and their friends and family members, court staff, and attorneys. Court staff may include clerks, bailiffs, interpreters, and service providers.

Overall

Judge McIff received positive reviews from the observer and all respondents. The judge presented as a methodical, calm judge who made sure that participants understood their rights and responsibilities in court. He allowed them to express themselves and was attentive to their needs.

What most people said

The observer reported that the judge’s thorough nature and skill set demonstrated the epitome of consistency. He took seriously the responsibility to ensure all in court, particularly those without counsel, understood their rights, and he conveyed a conscientious, careful approach in doing so. He verified that all litigants had reviewed and signed the rights form (or watched the rights video), and he always ensured they had no questions at any point in the process. He was happy to explain information, such as potential ramifications for charges, how to fill out the rights form, or the process for how one would represent themself. In one matter, he made sure a litigant knew what they were supposed to do for their next appointment in court. With participants living outside of Utah, he was careful to make them aware of potential time zone changes for their future appointments. In one instance, he was thorough in helping a litigant know what to expect if they were out of the country for their next court date.

The observer found that Judge McIff exhibited a courteous, calm manner and came across as reasonable and approachable, yet confident and unflappable. He seemed easy to talk to and able to put litigants at ease. At the conclusion of the hearings, he routinely thanked all parties and wished them a good day. In one instance, after having addressed the case of an inmate from a local jail, the judge expressed appreciation to both the inmate and the official who put the inmate on WebEx. “I appreciate that,” he said to the official. He allowed participants to express themselves at the right moment, and he appeared to listen intently. In one matter, the judge asked, “Is there anything else you haven’t told me that you want to tell me?” He then allowed them to talk. One litigant took the opportunity to tell the judge about the auto accident that occurred when he was charged with the infraction, and the Judge visibly paid attention.

Interview Summary: Five of the 14 respondents particularly emphasized Judge McIff's reasonable nature and concern for individual needs and situations. All five described the judge as “understanding.” One found that he was “very attentive” to court staff and that he “tries to understand” the situations and needs of participants in court. Another reported that he “understands” that litigants “are not perfect,” and he doesn’t hold that against them, while another appreciated that it “didn’t feel like he [the judge] wanted to rein it down on you.”

Five highlighted the judge’s courteous demeanor. They variously described him as “nice,” straightforward, and “firm.” One stated that he “wasn’t judgy,” while another added, “he respected what I had so say.”

Four underscored the judge’s neutrality. One described him as “very consistent,” while another observed that he follows the law and “doesn’t let his emotions get the best of him.” Another shared, “he treats everybody the same.”

Two commented favorably on the judge’s listening skills. One said, “he listened to what I had to say,” while the other noted, “he listens to everything that is being said.”

Biography (from JPEC)

Judge Mark McIff was first appointed to the bench in May 2014. He currently serves as Justice Court judge for Sevier County, Piute County, Fairview, Fountain Green, Spring City, Moroni, Ephraim City, Gunnison, Manti, and Mt. Pleasant. He received a bachelor's degree in Accounting in 1995 from Southern Utah University and a Juris Doctorate in 1998 from Gonzaga University in Spokane, Washington. Prior to his appointment to the bench, Judge McIff served as the Piute County Attorney, the Wayne County Attorney, Deputy Sevier County Attorney, Deputy Garfield County Attorney, and Chief Criminal Prosecutor for the Wasatch County Attorney's Office. He also served as the city prosecutor and city attorney for several cities. In addition, Judge McIff maintained a private law practice in Richfield during most of his career.
