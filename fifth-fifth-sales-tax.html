The Fifth-Fifth Sales Tax

By Dr. Ronald Mortensen, Ph.D. · Co-Founder, Citizens for Tax Fairness

Posted Sept. 15, 2026 · Updated Oct. 17, 2026 (page restored; colors updated to match the docket)

Talking points opposing Davis County's proposed 0.20% (two-cents-per-ten-dollars) county-wide sales tax increase, prepared by a local tax-fairness advocate ahead of a commission vote.

Author contact: citizensfortaxfairness.org@gmail.com

About this document. Written and prepared by Dr. Ronald Mortensen, Co-Founder of Citizens for Tax Fairness, and reproduced here in full with attribution as a resource for readers and candidates. This is his own set of talking points and arguments, not Weber County Hive reporting or analysis — the figures, characterizations, and opinions below are his.
  • When the People are already struggling to cover the rising costs of clothing, housing, food, taxes, utilities, school supplies, insurance, etc., elected officials should be looking for ways to reduce their tax burden, not increase it.
  • Sales taxes drive up inflation. If adopted, the proposed sales tax will immediately inflate the price of every taxable item by 0.20% and the county-wide sales tax will increase to 7.35%. When the price of a car goes up by $2,000, after the sales tax is added the total increase will be $2,147.
  • According to the Wasatch Front Regional Council, the new tax of only 2 cents on each $10 will generate $15,982,529 in new tax revenue. What the Council doesn't say is that it costs each of the 384,000 residents of Davis County $41.62 per year ($166 for a family of four) — and it doesn't say how many gallons of milk, how much fresh produce, or how many pounds of hamburger a family of four could buy with that $166.
  • This is a bait-and-switch proposal, since the county and cities get some unrestricted revenue for three years and then all of the revenue goes to transportation, including a full 50% to the Utah Transit Authority (UTA).
  • This is just another example of the governor and legislature's Pontius Pilate approach to taxation — legislators pass and the governor signs bills that incentivize (bribe) or even require local elected officials to raise taxes, while at the same time bragging about lowering the state income tax by pennies for the average Utahn.
  • As Commissioner Lorene Kamalu noted, Davis County households are already paying $400 annually for transit and not getting much for their money. This new tax will increase that amount to around $566 annually.
  • Commissioner Bob Stephenson's assertion that commissioners who don't support the tax increase are "not doing your duty with what you were sworn to office to do" is very troubling. He seems to see his role as always putting governments' needs ahead of those of the People.
  • City managers making $200K and $250K believe that increasing the sales tax is "a no-brainer," and that citizens will vote for the increase if it is put on the ballot.
  • Lame-duck commissioners who have already raised taxes multiple times, and shown that they don't know how to handle one-time funds, should not be raising taxes during the waning days of their tenure.
  • The Court Facilities Director told the Standard-Examiner that county attorneys will be able to stay in their current location until construction of the new courthouse is completed; therefore, this tax increase is not needed for new office space.
  • Commissioners need to step up and let the legislature and governor know that they stand with the People — not with unelected, overpaid city managers, the Wasatch Front Regional Council, mayors and city councils who never saw a tax increase they didn't like, or those trying to route taxpayer funds into 2034 Olympic-related projects. Citizens are already facing multiple property tax increases this year and don't need this added on top of those. County commissioners should NOT approve this additional sales tax.
