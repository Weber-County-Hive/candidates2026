Nearly Even Totals, Different Sources

Sen. Emily Buss of Eagle Mountain, the only Forward Party member of the Legislature, was appointed to this seat in December 2025. She faces Republican Brooks Benson. Each campaign reports about $35,800 to $35,900 in cash raised. Most of Buss’s came from her own party. Most of Benson’s came from PACs, businesses and the campaign committees of sitting Republican senators. The district includes Eagle Mountain, home of Meta’s data center campus.

Five Things the Records Show

IN PLAIN WORDSTwo people are on the ballot for this state Senate seat. They have raised almost the same amount of cash. Who gave it is very different.

  1. Buss reports $35,803.36 in cash and $12,894.06 in in-kind help. The Forward Party of Utah gave $25,999 in cash (72.6% of her cash) and $7,628.06 in in-kind help, including $6,446.51 listed only as “Data.” Her campaign spent $567.51 through the Primary Report.
  2. Benson reports $35,900 raised and $18,990.92 spent through the Primary Report. About 90% of his money came in 15 checks from PACs, businesses and campaign committees, including $5,000 from Senate Majority Leader Kirk Cullimore’s campaign and $3,000 from the Doers Network. Individuals gave $3,550.
  3. Benson files as “David Benson.” His disclosure form is signed “David Brooks Benson,” and his report lists a $4,451.50 travel payment to “Brooks Benson.” The Doers Network’s $3,000 to “David Benson,” which this site’s Doers file listed without a race, is this campaign.
  4. None of the six bills Buss sponsored in 2026 passed. Four bills she floor-sponsored or co-sponsored were signed, including a firefighter cancer fund and higher nicotine taxes. She took office Dec. 17, 2025, weeks before the session.
  5. Both candidates say growth should pay its own way. Buss has called for impact fees “that reflect the real cost development places on our strained infrastructure.” Benson’s site says impact fees “cover a fraction” of the cost and that AI data centers are projected to need far more power. In the sources read, only Benson addresses data centers directly.

EAGLE MOUNTAIN & META

The Data Center in This District

IN PLAIN WORDSMeta’s big data center is in Eagle Mountain, which both campaigns list as part of this district. Whoever wins will vote on data center and power rules.

  • By Meta’s own 2024 figures, the Eagle Mountain campus used more than 1 million megawatt-hours of electricity and about 35 million gallons of water. The full story is in this site’s Meta campus file (Part 1).
  • A gas plant to power it is before the Public Service Commission in docket 26-2660-01. See Part 2: the gas plant.
  • Buss lives in Eagle Mountain and sits on its city library’s advisory board. No statement by her on the data center was found in the sources read.
  • Benson’s energy page names AI data centers as a driver of power demand and backs more supply, including natural gas and nuclear.
  • Benson received $800 from a lobbying firm whose principal lists Meta as a client. Buss’s campaign paid Meta $12.88 for advertising. Neither is a sign of a position on the data center.
