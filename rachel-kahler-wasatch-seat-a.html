WASATCH COUNTY COUNCIL — SEAT A (AT-LARGE) · FOUR-CANDIDATE FIELD

What the Park Email Left Out

Four candidates are on the ballot for Seat A. Here is what each one's disclosure form says, and what the public record shows next to it.

Published Sept. 1, 2026 · Updated Oct. 2, 2026: added every candidate exchange in full; Sept. 30: McNeill’s Sept. 1 declaration, the state disclosure law, Hewlett regraded on her updated form

Rachel Kahler is the Republican nominee for Wasatch County Council Seat A on the November 2026 ballot. She won a contested June 2026 primary and is also the paid Executive Director of the Community Alliance for Main Street (CAMS), a $39,900-a-year, three-year contract position funded by Heber City.

The Democratic nominee has changed since this piece was first published: Patrick Saucier withdrew his candidacy on Aug. 31, 2026 to take a new job in his home state of New Hampshire, and the Wasatch County Democratic Party replaced him with Robert Duncan McNeill on Sept. 1. Two write-in candidates, Jami Hewlett and Shawn Taylor, joined the race after the write-in filing period closed Aug. 28. All four candidates' records are covered below, in the order their material actually developed.

In her CAMS capacity, Kahler recently sent an email to a small group of recipients asking them to organize a letter-writing campaign against Heber City's Phase 2 redesign of Main Street Park — and to keep the effort off social media.

"I am encouraging the public to participate in a professional email campaign, asking the City Council to reconsider the Main Street Park Phase 2 redesign plan. ... Please DO NOT put this information on Social Media Networks."

— Rachel Kahler, Executive Director, Community Alliance for Main Street, in an email obtained by The Weber County Hive

What the email asked for

The email lays out talking points for residents to send to Heber City Council (ccpublic@heberut.gov) opposing the current Phase 2 concept for Main Street Park — which Terracon Consultants has estimated at $15–18 million, funded through Utah Outdoor Recreation Grants, Impact Fees, and the local Trails, Arts and Parks (TAP) sales tax. The proposed amenities include a splash pad, a skate-and-ice ribbon, and an all-abilities playground.

Kahler's alternative: split the amenities off to a different, city-owned parcel known as the Duke property, near 500 North and 550 East, citing parking and winter-access concerns at Main Street Park. The email includes a full set of ready-made talking points for recipients to send as their own letters — all built around the same argument.

The email cites two pieces of supporting data: a Placer.ai commercial visitor-analytics report on Main Street Park, and a map of the Duke property. Both were independently verified against the source documents.

The claim that does the most work

The single sentence doing the heaviest lifting in the email is this one, explaining why the Duke property is the better site: it "is also adjacent to the Ritchie Group apartment development currently under construction, which is expected to include approximately 600 residential units and bring more than 2,000+ residents to the immediate area."

That development is Station Seven, a Ritchie Group project at the intersection of US-40 and Heritage Farms Parkway — the same site near Smith's referenced in the email's own map. Here is what the public record actually shows about it, as of the most recent reporting:

Kahler's email The public record
"Approximately 600 residential units" 245–267 units, per the developer's own filings with Heber City's Community Reinvestment Agency (Park Record, Oct.–Nov. 2025)
"Currently under construction" Not yet approved or under construction. Heber's CRA board narrowly backed a tax-sharing framework 3–2 in November 2025; first delivery was targeted for 2027, with construction phasing in over five years after that. Heber City, Wasatch County, and the Wasatch County School Board all still had to enter a required interlocal agreement — as of the most recent reporting, none had

Not mentioned in the email at all: Station Seven's retail and hotel components were, as of late 2025, contingent on a still-undecided UDOT highway route through the area. UDOT selected its preferred route in January 2026 in a way that leaves the project intact, though that route has since drawn enough public opposition that UDOT is considering redesigning it — meaning even the site's own future was not settled at the time the email describes it as a done deal.

Even the tax-sharing vote that got Station Seven this far was contested on the record: Councilmember Yvonne Barney voted no, saying the CRA "is meant for central Heber, not the area around Smith's," and Mayor Heidi Franco later called annexing the property "the one decision she regrets" making in office.

What the visitor data actually shows

The email's Placer.ai attachment is real — a paid commercial analytics report, not a casual pull, showing genuine attendance spikes at Main Street Park on event days. But the same report complicates the "overwhelmed local park" framing rather than supporting it outright: a meaningful share of visits come from well outside Heber — American Fork, Pleasant Grove, Wallsburg, Bountiful, Eagle Mountain, and other zip codes up to 30+ miles away. That's a park drawing a regional crowd on event days, not simply a neighborhood amenity being overrun by its own residents.

What the disclosure form actually asks

Kahler's Wasatch County candidacy filing, submitted with her January 2026 Declaration of Candidacy, checked "I have no conflict of interest to report" and left every supporting line blank. The form itself asks candidates to describe:

"...other county, nonprofit, and for-profit boards you (and your spouse) sit on, any for-profit businesses for which you or an immediate family member are an officer, manager or director, or a majority shareholder, and the name of your employer and any businesses you or a family member own..."

— Wasatch County Conflict of Interest Disclosure Form
Form line What should have appeared
Employer CAMS (Community Alliance for Main Street)
Boards you sit on — "volunteer or otherwise" Red White & Blue Festival Inc. (Director), North Village Special Service District, Heber Light & Power Board — the latter two self-disclosed by Kahler in her own campaign messaging, not found via records research
For-profit businesses — officer, manager, director, or majority shareholder 5 In The Hive, LLC (Manager)
Businesses you or a family member own 5 In The Hive, LLC

Six real entries — built entirely from her own state filings, her own CAMS contract, and her own campaign posts. Every one was left blank on the form itself.

Left off that form: her paid CAMS directorship, a directorship of Red White and Blue Festival Inc., sole management of 5 In The Hive, LLC — an entity whose 2023 Certificate of Organization, which she signed, states its purpose as being "to acquire, own, sell, and otherwise manage real estate investments" — and, per her own campaign messaging, service on the North Village Special Service District and the Heber Light & Power Board.

The form's opening language does leave room for judgment — it asks candidates to list only what they "believe could contribute to a conflict of interest." So the question isn't simply whether the form was filled out correctly; it's whether concluding that an employer, two special-district/utility boards, a nonprofit board seat, and an owned LLC couldn't contribute to a conflict was a reasonable call for someone seeking a seat that votes on land use and zoning.

"I do not consider my employment with CAMS, by itself, to constitute a conflict of interest with service on the Wasatch County Council... 5 In The Hive, LLC... is not an operating business that contracts with Wasatch County."

— Rachel Kahler, in a public response to this reporting

Kahler's response describes 5 In The Hive, LLC as her and her siblings' childhood home, held jointly after their parents' deaths and rented long-term, including below-market rates to seasonal workers. That's her account of how the entity is actually used today — it doesn't change what she filed with the state in 2023, which is the record above. Both are part of the story: what she filed, and what she now says about it.

Her response doesn't address why CAMS — her employer, by the form's own category — her Red White & Blue Festival board seat, or her North Village SSD/Heber Light & Power service, all covered by the form's language, were left off entirely.

A same-seat comparison

Luke Searle, the outgoing Seat A councilman Kahler is running to replace, filed the identical county form about a year earlier. He checked the opposite box — "I have the following conflict of interest to report" — and listed his employer, Wasatch County School District, and his wife's business, Heber Balloons. The form isn't unusually hard to fill out; the county's own outgoing officeholder for this exact seat did it without incident.

A form built to say less

Utah Code §17-70-304 requires county clerks to provide candidates a conflict-of-interest disclosure form "divided into sections representing each item of information described in Subsections 20A-11-1604(6)(a) through (n)" — with a response space immediately beneath each section. That's a comprehensive, itemized form, not a single page. The same law says the statement is filed with the Declaration of Candidacy, that the clerk may not accept a declaration until it receives a complete statement, and that the clerk posts it on the county website.

What that's supposed to look like is easy to see: Utah's own template for state-level candidates runs to eight sections and roughly thirteen individual items — current and prior employers, owned or officered entities, $5,000-or-more income sources, stocks and bonds, paid and unpaid board positions, real property interests, a spouse's information, other household adults, and any other conflict. The form Kahler filed for her Wasatch County seat has one checkbox and three blank lines.

There's also a separate legal duty that sits with the county itself. Under the parallel enforcement framework built into state law, a filing clerk who determines that a submitted disclosure doesn't meet the statutory content requirements must, within five days, notify the officer of the violation and direct them to file a corrected version. The gap here isn't only what Kahler wrote on the form — it's what the form itself was ever built to ask, and whether anyone at the county was checking.

Jami Hewlett

Hewlett filed as a write-in candidate for Seat A on Aug. 26, 2026, days after the write-in filing period opened. This is her fourth run for Heber-area office (also 2023, 2024 for Wasatch County Council and Heber City Council).

Her Wasatch County Conflict of Interest Disclosure Form, also dated Aug. 26, 2026, checks the opposite box from Kahler's — "I have the following conflict of interest to report" — and lists three items: "My husband owns Hewlett Life & Health — Health Insurance and Life Insurance," "I own Summit Cleaning," and "I have a lot on 100 S. in Heber." Item two independently checks out: Utah's business registry confirms Summit Cleaning, LLC (entity #9699050-0160, formed 2016, active) with Hewlett as both registered agent and principal, listed under her full legal name, Jami Smith. The other two items — Hewlett Life & Health and the 100 S. property — haven't yet been cross-checked against state filings or the county assessor.

Not on that first form: Twelve Point Muley LLC (entity #13150857-0160, formed 2022, active), a four-member LLC that lists Hewlett as a Member alongside Bart Smith, Jennifer Jolley, and Bret Smith.

Updated Sept. 30, 2026: Hewlett fixed her form within days. On Sept. 2, 2026, one week after her first filing, nine days before this outlet first reported on the LLC, and with no record of anyone pointing it out to her first, she filed an updated Conflict of Interest Disclosure Form. It adds "Twelve Point Muley 1/4 interest in apartment in Wyoming (inheritance)" and "Wasatch Back Handyman LLC owns 191 W 100 S." The county's page still shows only her Aug. 26 form. When she wrote to this outlet, she explained that the LLC holds a Wyoming apartment she inherited with her siblings, confirmed the details, and did not dispute the record. Her letter is published in full: "In Her Own Words: Her Updated Disclosure."

Also on the public record

  • Nov. 2024: removed 4–1 from the Heber City Planning Commission (an alternate seat) after distributing a letter opposing a downtown redevelopment plan under the name of a local business owner, Winston Lee of Lee Music, without his consent — reported by KSL, Park Record, and KPCW. Lee said the letter did not represent him; Hewlett has said her intent was to promote transparency and that the letter was "mischaracterized."
  • Multiple pending criminal cases reported by Park Record (June 2025), unrelated to county disclosure or governance — not detailed here, as this outlet's rubric grades governance and disclosure conduct, not personal legal matters.

Robert McNeill

McNeill, a Democrat and Heber City resident since 2020, replaced Patrick Saucier on the ballot Sept. 1, 2026 after Saucier withdrew to take a new job in New Hampshire. McNeill is an actor, director, and producer — best known for playing Lt. Tom Paris on "Star Trek: Voyager" — and studied theater at The Juilliard School; he says he's directed roughly 200 television episodes and produced roughly 1,000. He's a political newcomer — a "freshman," in his own words, with no prior elected experience.

His platform centers on growth ("the No. 1 issue," in his words), accountability, and transparency. He's proposed a formal "response process" giving the public time to weigh in on major development issues beyond standard public-comment periods, and says the County Council "has consistently shown its cards" on transparency but could do more. (Park Record, "Wasatch County Council candidate Robert McNeill values transparency, accountability," Sept. 11, 2026.)

McNeill's Declaration of Candidacy was sworn and accepted by the county's filing officer on Sept. 1, 2026. It covers the standard nomination paperwork (residency, party affiliation, campaign-finance obligations). No conflict-of-interest statement is in that packet, and none is posted on the county's Conflict of Interest Disclosure Forms page as of Sept. 30, 2026 (both read from the rendered pages). No campaign finance report has been located yet either.

State law says a county clerk "may not accept a declaration of candidacy" for a county office until the clerk receives a complete conflict-of-interest disclosure statement (Utah Code §17-70-304(4)). The exception for incumbents seeking reelection does not apply to him. Whether the county received a statement from McNeill and has not posted it is a question for the Clerk/Auditor's office.

One business is confirmed tied to him on the state registry: Here We Go Productions, Inc., based in Heber City — his own production company, consistent with his real production credits (Executive Producer/Director on "Resident Alien," among other credits). There's no COI form yet to check it against, since none has been filed. That will be revisited as his filings develop.

McNeill's report card

Not gradable yet. McNeill has no board seats, votes, or disclosure filings on record. His production company is confirmed but has nothing to be checked against, since he hasn't filed a COI form yet — there is nothing to measure against any of the five criteria. This is a function of when he entered the race, not a mark against him.

Shawn Taylor

Taylor, from Charleston (a small town within Wasatch County), filed his write-in candidacy for Seat A on Aug. 27, 2026. He ran for Charleston mayor in 2025, placing third in both the primary and general election. Married to Addie, with four children; he says he's lived in Charleston more than nine years and grew up in rural Utah around ranching, livestock, and irrigation. His campaign materials describe him and his wife as having "built several businesses in a variety of markets," without naming any of them, and list him as Charleston's GOP precinct vice chair, a member of the NFIB (National Federation of Independent Business) State Legislative Council, a former state delegate and precinct chair, and — per his 2026 campaign site — a current member of the Wasatch County GOP Board, serving as Media Specialist.

His Wasatch County Conflict of Interest Disclosure Form, filed alongside his write-in candidacy, checks "I have no conflict of interest to report." That board seat doesn't appear on the form, though a party-organizing role carries no obvious financial or personal stake in a county vote, and Charleston's population is small enough (roughly 500 people) that it's plausibly common knowledge rather than a concealed interest. No specific business has been named to check against the "no conflict" answer on financial grounds; the "several businesses" claim isn't attached to any company this outlet can independently verify. No campaign finance filing has been located yet for this county race.

Hello. I’m not sure what your interest in Wasatch County is, or who you really are. But since you’re so interested in me, I’ll share the details you didn’t have the time to research.

As a candidate for the Wasatch County Council, I believe transparency regarding my professional roles is important. I serve as Executive Director of the Community Alliance for Main Street (CAMS), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization dedicated to revitalizing downtown Heber City through historic preservation, economic development, beautification, and community-centered programming.

CAMS holds a three-year contract with Heber City to provide event management and related services. That agreement is between Heber City and CAMS as an independent nonprofit organization. The contract is not with me personally, nor do I personally hold the contract.

For that reason, I do not consider my employment with CAMS, by itself, to constitute a conflict of interest with service on the Wasatch County Council. It is, however, a professional relationship that I am fully willing to disclose in the interest of transparency.

Should a specific matter come before the County Council involving CAMS in a way that could create an actual or potential financial or professional conflict for me, I would disclose that relationship and follow all applicable County policies and Utah law regarding participation or recusal.

As part of my professional responsibilities with CAMS, I have applied for and administered a number of competitive, program-based grants during the past two years. These grants have been awarded to CAMS, not to me personally, and are designated for specific projects, programs, professional development, marketing, equipment, or contracted services.

If elected, I would distinguish between my responsibilities as Executive Director of CAMS and my responsibilities as a member of the County Council. If CAMS were to submit a funding request, grant application, contract, or other matter requiring County Council action, I would disclose my professional relationship with CAMS and follow applicable state law and County policies governing participation and recusal.

I serve as a volunteer with the Red, White & Blue Festival, a nonprofit organization established to organize and produce a patriotic community celebration in Heber City in recognition of Independence Day and the Fourth of July.

My involvement with the organization is entirely voluntary, and I receive no salary, stipend, or other personal compensation for my service. My involvement is solely as a community volunteer working with others to provide an Independence Day celebration for residents and visitors to the Heber Valley.

The LLC provides the structure through which our family jointly owns and manages the property. It is not an operating business that contracts with Wasatch County.
