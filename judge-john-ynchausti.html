Hive judge-rubric grades

How to read the grades: A C means the record meets what Utah law requires, the status quo. A B or A means the judge went beyond it, and the record shows it. D means the judge passed JPEC’s minimum but JPEC rates that area below the judge’s peers; F means below JPEC’s legal minimum score of 3.6. Every grade is taken from JPEC’s own published wording, the same way for all 57 judges. No grade is based on how any case came out, or on who appointed the judge.

What JPEC’s report says

The text below is JPEC’s own public report, reproduced as published. Quotes inside it are from JPEC’s survey respondents and courtroom observers, not from the Hive.

JPEC summary

Survey Respondent Recommendation:95% of survey respondents recommend Judge Ynchausti for retention

Performance Measures: Survey scores are analyzed for statistical significance to determine if the judge scored well above, above, consistent with, below, or well below their justice court peers. Above Peers: Legal Ability, Integrity & Judicial Temperament, Administrative Skills, Procedural Fairness

Respondent and Observer Feedback:Both respondents and courtroom observers praise Judge Ynchausti’s levelheaded temperament and respectful, dignified treatment of court participants.

Both appreciate his impartiality and strong commitment to the law.

Respondents further say that he is capable and prepared, while observers highlight the judge’s sensitivity to participant’s needs.

All four observers report confidence that they would be treated fairly if they were to appear in Judge Ynchausti’s court.

Courtroom observers, overall

  • Together, the four observers were strongly positive about Judge Ynchausti.
  • All observers reported that if they appeared before Judge Ynchausti they would be treated fairly.

What observers agreed on

  • Observers variously reported that Judge Ynchausti worked efficiently through the docket and modeled “a strong grasp of the law across a variety of cases.” He presented a calm, polite, and reassuring demeanor, and he showed respect by thanking defendants and trying to pronounce their names right. He operated in an unhurried, careful way and “did not sacrifice attention to each of the defendants.” The judge sought and considered input from participants and allowed them “to explain their situations in their own words,” in one matter, allowing a defendant to make an “almost five-minute statement” after pleading guilty. He explained rights, legal terms and orders, and “asked individuals if they understood.”
  • All observers particularly emphasized the judge’s consistency and neutrality. They variously described him as even-handed, impartial, and as one who safeguards individual rights. One observer reported that his decisions “were rooted in legal standards rather than assumptions or personal opinion,” and another stated that he made sure defendants knew the rights they gave up “when pleading guilty” and did so “without coercion.”
  • All highlighted the judge’s demonstrated concern for participants’ needs. When addressing fine payments with a defendant, one observed that he “double checked with them that the amount was not too much of a hardship to their budget.” Another observer noted a defendant’s worry about being properly excused from her work shift, but the judge “[reassured] her” and “demonstrated consideration” by making sure she received an excusal note. To another, the judge “[showed] an awareness of how intimidating the process might feel” when assisting a woman in a divorce case.

Other observations

  • Two observers spoke positively about the judge’s ability to listen and focus. Both reported that he listened, with one stating that he “provided attention to each of the defendants” and the other finding that he was “attentive…and responsive to any questions.”

Biography (from JPEC)

Judge J.C. Ynchausti was appointed to the Davis County Justice Court in 2016. Judge Ynchausti is the former Bountiful Assistant City Attorney and Prosecutor. He graduated from the University of Utah with degrees in History and Italian. He was honored by the University of Utah, College of Humanities as an outstanding undergraduate and by the Utah Historical Society as its Outstanding History Student. He attended the University of Idaho College of Law where he was awarded the Langroise Scholarship and served as a Senior Class member of the Conduct Review Board, a student representative of the Tenure Review Committee, and Treasurer of Delta Theta Phi. He is licensed to practice law in both Idaho and Utah. He was employed as the law clerk for Idaho Fifth District Judge James J. May in Sun Valley, Idaho. As a civil practitioner he has worked in the fields of adoption, employment law, family law and general litigation. He has served on several Utah State Bar committees and has been a mediator for the Utah Equal Employment Opportunity Anti-Discrimination Division. For almost 25 years he was appointed, by the Second District Court, to represent patients in the civil commitment process. He has also served as an instructor on the topic of civil commitment for the Davis County Behavioral Health Crisis Intervention Team Program. As an assistant Bountiful City attorney his primary responsibilities were to assist the city attorney, advise the Board of Adjustment and Traffic Safety Committee. As a prosecutor his primary responsibilities included, but were not limited to, advising, training and assisting police, and prosecuting all misdemeanor crimes. He has trained prosecutors locally and from around the country at the National District Attorney's Association. An enthusiastic ice hockey player, Judge Ynchausti has also served as the USA Hockey/Utah Amateur Hockey Association Safe Sport Coordinator. In 2022 the Board of Justice Court Judges presented Judge Ynchausti with the Justice Court Service Award for his outstanding efforts on behalf of Utah’s Justice Courts and for furthering the professional and legal competence of Utah Justice Court Judges. In 2025 the Board of Justice Court Judges recognized Judge Ynchausti with the Justice Court Outstanding Service Award in recognition of his service as the Education Liaison between Justice Court Judges and the Utah Judiciary. In 2026 the Board of Justice Court Judges recognized the Davis County Justice Court as the Outstanding Justice Court for continued excellence in serving the public.
