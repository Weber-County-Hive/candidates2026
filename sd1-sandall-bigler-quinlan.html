Six Things the Records Show

IN PLAIN WORDSThree people are running for this state Senate seat. One is a longtime senator with a large campaign account. The other two report very little money. Everything on this page comes from public filings the candidates made themselves, plus the Legislature’s own bill records.

  1. Sandall’s 2026 reports list $67,651 raised and $115,013 spent. He started 2026 with money from 2025: his 2025 Year End report lists $95,857 raised and $59,197 spent.
  2. The largest share of his 2026 spending, $43,105, is listed as “Donations.” That includes $9,000 each to three other Republican legislative campaigns (Thomas Petersen, Chris Wilson and Casey Snider).
  3. $30,299 is listed as “Travel Expenses,” including hotels in Zurich, Berlin, Oslo and India. The filings name the payee, not the purpose of each trip.
  4. He received $45,425 from 33 contributors between Oct. 3 and Oct. 6. These sit on the state’s temporary “Received Contributions” list until his General Report is filed (due Oct. 27).
  5. His conflict-of-interest form lists four family businesses and one board seat: “Chair – Utah Fairpark Area Investment and Restoration District.”
  6. Bigler and Quinlan report small campaigns. Bigler’s 2026 file has two expense lines ($217.73) and no itemized contributions. Most of Quinlan’s $7,379 is in-kind help from the Utah Forward Party.

IN THEIR OWN WORDS

Where They Stand

IN PLAIN WORDSWhat each candidate has said in public, with who reported it. The Salt Lake Tribune sent all three a questionnaire; Sandall and Bigler did not respond.

TOPIC
WHAT WAS SAID
Stratos data center (Box Elder)
Sandall declined KSL interviews and sent a statement that he will “watch closely to make sure any and all permits are properly vetted.” Bigler called it “a provisional ‘no’ until more study was done.” Quinlan: “No data centers in the greater Great Salt Lake basin.” (KSL, July 18, 2026)
MIDA
Quinlan said every candidate should pledge to dissolve MIDA. Bigler said she was not sure. (KSL, July 18, 2026)
Amendments A and B
Quinlan opposes both (Tribune, Oct. 12). Sandall and Bigler did not answer the Tribune.
Voting maps
Quinlan says she is running for fair maps and says Sandall worked against that effort. That is her claim, as reported by the Tribune; Sandall co-chaired the 2021 and 2025 redistricting committees (Deseret News).
Water and the Great Salt Lake
Quinlan (her site): require all water districts to conserve and restrict development that threatens the lake’s inflow. Sandall carried several water bills in the Senate in 2026 (see Bills).
