Katy Hall vs. Dave Calder

A registered nurse turned four-year incumbent, funded almost entirely by out-of-district PACs and industry committees, against a first-time Air Force veteran running on small-dollar and family support. Washington Terrace and Riverdale, Weber and Davis counties.

What the records show

Hall's campaign has raised roughly $77,800 across the 2022–2026 cycles, the large majority of it from PACs, industry committees, and legislator-to-legislator transfers rather than individual constituents. 01 The race

Katy Hall, a registered nurse, has represented House District 11 since defeating incumbent Kelly Miles in the 2022 Republican primary. She faces Dave Calder, a retired Air Force officer and mechanical engineer making his first run for office, in the November general election. A third Democrat, Josh R. Koskan, filed and then withdrew before the June 23 primary, leaving both candidates unopposed within their own parties this cycle.

The district covers Washington Terrace and Riverdale, spanning Weber and Davis counties.

02 Hall's legislative record

Hall's signature 2026 legislation was HB 51, Adoption Amendments, which she primary-sponsored after a former colleague flagged a rise in what she described as transaction-like adoptions; the bill passed the House 66–1 and is publicly framed as strengthening protections for birth parents.

In 2024 she was primary sponsor of HB 261, Equal Opportunity Initiatives, which bars public education institutions and government employers from requiring diversity-related trainings, statements, or offices.

In 2023 she sponsored Utah's ban on gender-affirming care for transgender minors. A subsequent University of Utah Drug Regimen Review Center report found low risk associated with the standard hormonal treatments covered by the ban, and found that access to treatment was associated with reduced risk of youth suicide; Hall maintained that the ban had a scientific basis. She also joined 81 other state lawmakers and the American Family Association — designated a hate group by the Southern Poverty Law Center — on an amicus brief in United States v. Skrmetti that cited biblical verses, a position at odds with briefs filed by the American Academy of Pediatrics, the American Psychological Association, and the American Bar Association.

2026 session: a healthcare-heavy record

Of the nine bills Hall primary-sponsored in the 2026 General Session, seven touch healthcare, pharmacy, or hospital policy directly: Health Care Transparency Amendments, Pharmacy Pricing Amendments, Hospital Workplace Violence Reporting Requirements, Healthcare Worker Post-Employment Amendments, and the Adoption Amendments described above, alongside a school-athlete-protection bill and a school-activity resolution. She also floor-sponsored Health Insurance Preauthorization Amendments and three more healthcare bills — Pediatric Care, Veterinary Post-employment, and Anesthesia Dosage Amendments — each paired with Sen. Jen Plumb, a Democrat.

That concentration lines up with her professional background as a registered nurse. It also lines up with her donor base: this cycle Hall has taken PAC contributions from UnitedHealth Group, Johnson & Johnson PAC, PhRMA, the Utah Medical Political Action Committee, Utah HOSPAC, and Revere Health — all companies or associations with a direct stake in the healthcare policy she writes.

HB 273, Classroom Technology Amendments

Hall co-sponsored this bill (primary sponsor Ariel Defay, floor sponsor Chris Wilson), a guardrails measure that mandates the Utah State Board of Education adopt model AI/technology policies, sets grade-level screen-time limits, requires written parental notification before classroom AI use, bans AI from independently grading students or making high-stakes decisions about them, and imposes privacy-compliance requirements. It's the same bill later cited as the legislative basis for USBE's rollout of Google Gemini for Education to roughly 680,000 students — a rollout that proceeded without a full board vote. Whether that rollout actually complied with HB 273's own requirements remains an open question.

Cross-reference
HB 236, Truth in Taxation Amendments

Hall also co-sponsored HB 236 (chief sponsor Karen Peterson, floor sponsor Daniel McCay), which requires taxing entities proposing a property tax rate increase to produce a new "property tax impact schedule" — a disclosure document laying out the dollar and percentage impact — and to hold the additional revenue in a restricted budget account until final adoption. It also creates a one-year enforcement grace period for the fiscal year beginning July 2026: the State Tax Commission cannot deny a taxing entity's rate increase even if that entity skips the new requirement. Like HB 273, it's a mixed record — a new disclosure mandate paired with softened enforcement in its first year.

Cross-reference

Platform vs. record

Hall's own campaign site states she supports "market solutions like price transparency, reduced regulations, and telemedicine instead of government mandates or socialized medicine." It specifically credits her with HB 270 and SB 319 as bills that let patients "continue care seamlessly... without unnecessary delays or confusion" — and she did sponsor and co-sponsor both.

Worth reading side by side

Her platform frames her approach as favoring reduced regulation over government mandates. Her own primary-sponsored healthcare bills this session — Health Care Transparency Amendments, Hospital Workplace Violence Reporting Requirements, and Pharmacy Pricing Amendments — are themselves new reporting and disclosure requirements placed on the healthcare industry, not a reduction in regulation.

04 Conflicts of interest

Hall's own state-required conflict-of-interest disclosure lists a Fidelity brokerage account holding stock in 29 companies, including Boeing, Johnson & Johnson, and Nucor. All three also gave her campaign PAC money this cycle.

Stock & PAC overlap

Boeing PAC gave Hall $500; Johnson & Johnson PAC gave $600 across two cycles; Nucor Corporation PAC gave $1,800 across four separate contributions. Hall personally holds stock in all three companies, per her own disclosure.

Family medical-industry ties

Hall is a registered nurse at Ogden Regional Medical Center. Her spouse, Michael Hall, works at Ogden Clinic, holds ownership stakes in IHC Layton Surgical Center and North Ogden Surgical Center, and sits on the board at Ogden Regional Medical Center — the same hospital where Hall is employed.

Disclosure gap, dated

Hall's January 2026 conflict-of-interest filing disclosed her spouse's surgical-center ownership, his hospital board seat, and her own ownership in a separate LLC. Her July 2026 filing, submitted five months later, leaves that entire section blank.

05 Dave Calder

Calder is an Air Force veteran and mechanical engineer who worked as an aircraft battle-damage engineer at Hill Air Force Base before joining the University of Dayton Research Institute, where he remains employed as a Distinguished Research Engineer supporting aging-aircraft systems for military and commercial clients. A former Davis County Republican delegate, he says he left the party the day it nominated Donald Trump for president in 2016.

His conflict-of-interest disclosure shows no owned entities, no stock or bond holdings above the $5,000 reporting threshold, and a single unpaid board position on his neighborhood homeowners association.

His campaign has drawn support from Democratic nominees in neighboring districts, including Rosemary Lesser and her spouse (HD10) and Bianca Mittendorf (HD7), alongside donations from Washington Terrace neighbors and family.

His platform, in his own words

Calder's campaign site lists nine issue positions:

  • Affordable housing for young families, citing Wasatch Front median home prices more than doubling since 2015.
  • Healthcare access — supports Medicaid expansion to cover gaps left by federal ACA cuts.
  • Wages and prices — eliminate the state grocery tax; support living wages.
  • Protect fair elections — opposes the legislature's handling of Proposition 4, the voter-enacted measure barring congressional gerrymandering.
  • End state support for ICE activity he describes as unconstitutional, including opposition to a proposed ICE detention center in Utah.
  • Women's health — keep reproductive healthcare decisions free of government interference.
  • Protect vote by mail.
  • Protect higher education funding against budget cuts he characterizes as tied to "unscientific mandates."
  • Opposition to discrimination based on race, gender, or sexual orientation.
