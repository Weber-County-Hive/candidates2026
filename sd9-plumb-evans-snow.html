Five Things the Records Show

IN PLAIN WORDSThree people are running for this state Senate seat. Only one of them, the current senator, reports raising money. Here is what each one's filings say.

  1. Plumb raised $19,461 through Sept. 11. $13,450 of it (69%) came from seven PAC, business or association checks. $6,011 came from 32 individual entries, nearly all with Salt Lake City addresses.
  2. Her largest donor is a PAC she is listed as an officer of. The Senate Democratic caucus PAC (SenDemPAC) gave $10,000 on April 5. SenDemPAC's registration lists Jennifer Plumb as an officer. Its own reports list payments to her campaign that match.
  3. Evans's two reports show $0. His Convention and Primary reports list no contributions and no expenditures. No campaign website or bio was found.
  4. Snow's report lists $446.19 in spending and no contributions. It covers a filing fee, $300 to a communications firm and website hosting. The export doesn't show where that money came from.
  5. 12 of her 30 bills in 2026 became law, each with a Republican House sponsor. None of her housing bills passed, and a draft on opioid settlement money was abandoned. All three candidates filed disclosure forms with entries filled in: Plumb lists her medical jobs and five unpaid or nonprofit roles, including chair of the state's Opioid Settlement Advisory Committee. Snow lists Amazon Web Services as his employer and Amazon stock. Evans lists his cybersecurity job.

Two things worth seeing side by side

Plumb and SenDemPAC. SenDemPAC's state registration lists Jennifer Plumb as an officer, alongside Sen. Luz Escamilla and Sen. Kathleen Riebe. Her form's list of entities she is "an owner or officer" of doesn't include SenDemPAC. The form doesn't say whether a PAC counts as an "entity" for that item, and this page doesn't decide that.
