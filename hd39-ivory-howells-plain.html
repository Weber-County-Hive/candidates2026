IN PLAIN WORDS

HD-39: Ken Ivory vs. Drew Howells

Who is running? Rep. Ken Ivory, a Republican lawyer from West Jordan, has the seat. Drew Howells, a Democrat and retired veteran from Sandy, is running against him. The district is most of West Jordan and parts of Midvale and Sandy.

Where does Ivory’s money come from? In 2026 he raised about $66,000. About 60% came from two groups that fund House Republicans. About 23% came from people. Only 17 gifts came from West Jordan.

Where does Howells’s money come from? He raised about $38,000. About half came from 281 people, most giving $100 or less. Most of the rest came from women’s, environmental and union groups. He also lent his campaign $1,400.

The company Ivory paid most. In 2024, Ivory’s campaign paid a company called Veracity Enterprises about $40,000. State records say the company’s legal contact (its “registered agent”) is Stratton Law Group. Ivory’s own disclosure form says he works with that law firm. Another lawmaker, Sen. Keven Stratton, also paid the same company about $38,000. The records don’t say what the company did. In all, 15 campaigns and groups paid that company about $158,000 from 2022 to 2026, including another senator who carried Ivory’s gold bill and a State School Board member.

The book laws. Ivory wrote both of Utah’s school book laws. The 2024 one says that if three school districts remove a book, it comes out of every public school in Utah.

The book emails (a tip came in). A lawmaker can’t challenge a book under that law. In 2025, emails show Ivory asked a Jordan School Board member to challenge a book for him at two West Jordan high schools. Later he pushed another board member, Lisa Dean, about books. Lisa Dean ran against him in 2024.

Who backs them? Ivory: the House Speaker, the state treasurer, Sen. Stratton and Brooke Stephens, who runs a book-rating website. Howells: the teachers’ union, the Teamsters, Equality Utah and veterans groups.

Our grades. Money: Ivory C−, Howells C. Disclosure forms: both C+. Ivory’s book laws: B, C− and D. A C means about what the law requires. A D means a public check got weaker.
