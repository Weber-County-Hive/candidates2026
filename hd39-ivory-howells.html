Nine Things the Records Show

IN PLAIN WORDSKen Ivory has held this seat since 2023 and served in the House before that. Most of his 2026 money comes from two PACs that fund House Republicans. Drew Howells has never held office; his money comes from about 280 small gifts and Democratic-leaning groups. Both are checked the same way. Only Ivory has a voting record.

  1. Ivory raised $66,345.75 in 2026 through Oct. 22. $40,000 of it (60.3%) came from the Utah House Republican Election PAC ($20,000) and House Speaker Mike Schultz’s leadership PAC ($20,000). Individuals gave $15,390.19 in 35 gifts (23.2%). He reported $48,142.96 on hand on Oct. 22.
  2. Since 2022, those two PACs have given him $69,000 ($35,500 and $33,500). His largest individual donor is Howard Rich of Bala Cynwyd, Pa.: $18,000 in four gifts, plus $2,500 from the Howard Rich Revocable Trust in 2026. Goldback Inc of Alpine gave $7,500 on June 12, 2024.
  3. In 2024 his campaign paid Veracity Enterprises, LLC $40,364.37 in eight payments, $15,787.42 of it in the six weeks before his June 25, 2024 primary. State records list Veracity’s registered agent as Stratton Law Group PLLC. Ivory’s own disclosure form lists an “of counsel and cost sharing association with Stratton Law Group, PLLC.” Sen. Keven Stratton’s campaign paid Veracity $38,060.61 from 2022 to 2025. Stratton endorses Ivory on his campaign site.
  4. Fifteen campaigns and committees paid Veracity $158,318.72 from 2022 to 2026, including Sen. Keith Grover (Senate sponsor of Ivory’s 2026 gold-payment bill) and State School Board member Emily Green. Three payments are dated after the state lists the company as dissolved (April 21, 2026). No website for it was found.
  5. Ivory is the primary sponsor of both school book laws that passed, H.B. 374 (2022) and H.B. 29 (2024), and of H.B. 517 (2026), which died. Our Bill Tracker grades them B, C− and D.
  6. A tip came in with emails. In August 2025, Ivory asked Jordan School Board member Brian Barnett to file a challenge to Bag of Bones, since he is “not considered a person authorized” to do it himself. Barnett filed it at Copper Hills and West Jordan high schools. In November 2025 Ivory pressed board member Lisa Dean about removing books. BallotReady lists that same Lisa Dean as the person who ran against him in the 2024 primary.
  7. His campaign site lists Brooke Stephens, “RatedBooks.org founder,” as an endorser. She gave his campaign $50 on July 16, 2024, and was blind-copied on the August 2025 email to Barnett.
  8. Howells raised $38,362.70 in 2025–2026: $18,280.70 from 281 individual gifts (47.7%), 250 of them $100 or less; $19,082 from groups, led by Utah Women and Politics ($10,000) and Fresh Air PAC ($3,000); and $1,000 from the House Democratic caucus PAC. He also lent his campaign $1,400. He reported $5,864.64 on hand.
  9. Howells’s form lists him as retired, a homeowners association board vice president, and, after he amended it the next day, a board member of a veterans group, Dive Into Healing.

THE BOOK EMAILS

The Law’s Author Asked a Board Member to File

IN PLAIN WORDSThe 2024 law does not let a legislator challenge a book. Emails from a tip show Ivory asked a Jordan board member to file a challenge for him, at two West Jordan high schools.

A tip came in with emails. They are summarized on the Education page How a Book Gets Pulled From Every Utah School, which shows them step by step.

  1. Aug. 13, 2025: Brooke Stephens, founder of RatedBooks.org, emails school board members, legislators and the attorney general that Bag of Bones is in 12 schools.
  2. Aug. 14: Ivory asks Stephens whether “the auditors” have reached out about “the new audit on content.” The same afternoon he asks Jordan School Board member Brian Barnett to “review and submit this book for challenge inasmuch as I am not considered a person authorized to challenge such materials.” Stephens is blind-copied.
  3. Aug. 16: Barnett files a request to reconsider the book at Copper Hills and West Jordan high schools.
  4. Nov. 2025: Ivory forwards the state audit and a RatedBooks list of Jordan library titles to board member Lisa Dean, asking why parents have to search school by school. Stephens is copied.
  5. Feb. 2026: Bag of Bones becomes the 23rd book removed from every Utah public school (KPCW; Salt Lake Tribune). Which districts’ removals counted toward it is not shown in the records we have.
  • Lisa Dean: BallotReady lists Lisa Dean as a Jordan School Board member (District 7, 2022 to now) and as a 2024 HD-39 primary candidate. Ivory beat Dean 57.4% to 42.6% in that primary and 77.8% to 20.0% at the April 2026 convention; she later withdrew.
  • Brooke Stephens is on Ivory’s campaign endorsement page: “Parents need a fighter who values transparency and protection over deceptive rhetoric.”
  • Feb. 22, 2024: “Ken and Becky Ivory” signed the Deseret News open letter by Stephens and Gloria Vindas that points parents to RatedBooks.org.
  • A legislator can’t file a challenge under H.B. 29; a parent or board member has to. The emails show the law’s author asking a board member to file one.

Where words meet the record

  • “Fiscal Accountability”: Ivory’s filings itemize every gift and payment. His largest payee is a company whose registered agent is the law firm on his form; the filings list the payments only as “Campaign Expense” and “Advertising.”

RUBRIC

Grades on the Five Questions

IN PLAIN WORDSEvery item is graded on the same five questions: Power, Transparency, Financial accountability, Environmental impact and Community impact. A or B goes beyond what the law requires; C meets it; D means a public check is weakened or a key part is vague; F means required information is missing, or a check is removed with nothing in its place.

Overall is the average of the graded questions (A = 4, B = 3, C = 2, D = 1, F = 0; N/A and dashes left out). A plus or minus shows where the average falls; exactly halfway counts toward the higher letter with a minus. Only what has already happened is graded.
