Hive judge-rubric grades

How to read the grades: A C means the record meets what Utah law requires, the status quo. A B or A means the judge went beyond it, and the record shows it. D means the judge passed JPEC’s minimum but JPEC rates that area below the judge’s peers; F means below JPEC’s legal minimum score of 3.6. Every grade is taken from JPEC’s own published wording, the same way for all 57 judges. No grade is based on how any case came out, or on who appointed the judge.

What JPEC’s report says

The text below is JPEC’s own public report, reproduced as published. Quotes inside it are from JPEC’s survey respondents and courtroom observers, not from the Hive.

JPEC summary

Survey Respondent Recommendation:88% of survey respondents recommend Judge Neill for retention

Performance Measures: Survey scores are analyzed for statistical significance to determine if the judge scored well above, above, consistent with, below, or well below their juvenile court peers. Consistent with peers: Legal Ability, Integrity & Judicial Temperament, Administrative Skills, Procedural Fairness

Respondent and Observer Feedback:Respondents describe Judge Neill as fair, and they commend his caring for youth and commitment to working in the best interest of youth and their families.

Both respondents and courtroom observers admire his kindness, professionalism, and respectful treatment of those in court.

Two respondents indicate the judge could benefit from more training on the impacts of domestic violence.

All four observers report confidence that they would be treated fairly if they were to appear in Judge Neill’s court.

Courtroom observers, overall

  • Together, the four observers were positive about Judge Neill. One observer had an additional comment (see “Anomalous Comments”).
  • All observers reported that if they appeared before Judge Neill they would be treated fairly.

What observers agreed on

  • Observers variously reported that Judge Neill moved the calendar forward efficiently and appeared “well prepared for the hearings.” He fostered professionalism and respect by treating participants kindly and attending to their well-being, “[praising] all of the juveniles before him when they had done something positive.” He maintained neutrality by operating in a consistent, measured manner, and, in one matter, he advised an attorney and their client to talk out in the hall “so that he would not be swayed in any manner by their conversations.” The judge did not rush the proceedings, rescheduled a matter when he felt the juvenile was uncomfortable proceeding, and was “careful in addressing all courtroom participants.” He “took great pains” to ensure each person could express themselves, including the parents of the juveniles. He “explained clearly” decisions, rights, and “the ramifications of past actions” and verified that the youth understood.

Other observations

  • Two observers commented favorably on the judge’s body language, particularly his eye contact.
  • Two remarked positively on the courtroom atmosphere fostered by the judge. One called it “a good working atmosphere,” while the other described it as “purposeful.”
  • Two observed that he demonstrated concern for participants’ needs. One stated that he “[conveyed] a genuine concern” for those in court, and another noted that he was “conscientious about varying needs.”

Comments that stood apart

  • One observer described a hearing where the youth’s mother and grandparents made “inappropriate interjections” about who should get custody. The youth’s attorney then pointed out that custody was not the purpose of the hearing and that it “should not have been brought up.” The observer reported that the judge said nothing about this issue but felt that the judge “should have interceded and not left the discussion to be addressed by the attorney.”

Biography (from JPEC)

Judge Robert G. Neill was appointed to the Second District Juvenile Court by Gov. Gary R. Herbert in October 2016. He serves Davis, Morgan, and Weber counties. Judge Neill obtained a Bachelor of Arts degree from Brigham Young University and received his Juris Doctorate from the S.J. Quinney College of Law at the University of Utah. Prior to his appointment to the bench, Judge Neill worked as a Unit Chief in the Justice Division of the Salt Lake County District Attorney`s Office where he managed a variety of teams including the Screening and Misdemeanor Division, the Non-violent and General Felony Teams, the West Jordan Special Victim and Juvenile Teams, and the West Jordan Division. While at the District Attorney`s Office Judge Neill prosecuted numerous cases including crimes perpetrated against children and delinquency cases in the juvenile court.
