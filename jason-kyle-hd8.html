Case 02 · Utah House District 8 · Ogden Valley, Pleasant View, North Ogden & Mountain Green

The Career Total vs. The Six-Contribution Cycle

Rep. Jason Kyle (R) says he'll "fight against tax increases" and "protect your tax dollars." His own six-year fundraising record and 2026 legislative record tell a different story. Kim James (D) and Libby Shelton (Utah Forward Party) are also on the November ballot.

The three-way race: Jason Kyle (R, incumbent since 2023) vs. Kim James (D, retired United Methodist pastor) vs. Libby Shelton (Utah Forward Party). Kyle advanced from the 2026 Republican convention; James and Shelton also appear on the Weber County general-election ballot per the County Clerk's official candidate list.
What his campaign says vs. what the record shows: Kyle's campaign site (jasonbkyle.com) pledges he'll "fight against tax increases" and "protect your tax dollars." Going through his full sponsorship record and campaign finance history, we could not find a bill he authored, cosponsored, or voted on that actually delivers a tax cut or blocks a tax increase — and no donor pattern suggesting a specific tax fight he's waging on constituents' behalf. His one enacted tax bill (HB 300, below) is a school-district funding mechanism, not relief. Where this claim fits in his actual record is not something the public documents show; the bill docket below is the full record we found, for readers to check against the claim themselves.

The career-long money

Computed from the full itemized ledger across six reporting cycles (2018, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 through 6/11/26), excluding trivial credit-union interest credits: $79,736.39 raised total. Of that, $55,265.43 (69%) is PAC, corporate, or trade-association money across 95 separate line items; $17,055.86 (21%) is Kyle's own self-funding; only $7,415.10 (9%) came from other individuals.

The 2026 cycle: six contributions, zero from Weber County

Kyle's full 2026 itemized ledger, through the primary period, is just six real contributions (plus two trivial interest credits): Tourism Works PAC $250 (Monticello), Bridger Bolinder $1,000 (Grantsville — Tooele County, not Weber), Howard Rich $500 (Bala Cynwyd, PA), Utah Association of Realtors $1,500 (Sandy), Utah Bankers Association $500 (Salt Lake City), Boeing PAC $500 (Arlington, VA). None of the six donors is from Weber County — the district he represents.

By contrast, Kim James's 2026 cycle has more than 140 distinct contributions, dozens from named Weber-area individuals. The donor-count gap — 140+ vs. 6 — is the starkest single data point in the comparison.

The other candidates

Kim James (D)

Retired United Methodist pastor (Ogden First UMC, 2013–2025), 40 years in ministry across Colorado and Utah, lives on Ogden's East Bench. Campaign is heavily family- and small-dollar-funded: repeated in-kind and cash gifts from "Kimal James" and "Oscar James" (same home address as the candidate), plus a broad out-of-state small-dollar donor base consistent with a long ministry network. Received a one-time $7,500 gift from UINPAC (Utah Investment Network PAC, part of the Utah Donor Collaborative's multi-candidate Democratic slate) in the 2026 convention period — not a recurring funder; no PAC money appears in any subsequent report. Total 2026-cycle-to-date fundraising, combining all reports through the interim general-period ledger, runs well above $40,000, almost entirely small-dollar and local.

Libby Shelton (Utah Forward Party)

Full name per her own site: Libby Hernandez Shelton (CFIS/COI filings use Elizabeth Anne Shelton). Weber County resident 20+ years; works as a therapist specializing in substance use and domestic violence treatment (MSW, Utah State University; BA Anthropology, Weber State University). Politically active for 15 years as a state/county caucus delegate, including a decade as her local caucus's vice chair before switching to the Forward Party in 2025. Currently Weber County Vice-Chair of the Forward Party of Utah — a local party officer, not just a candidate. Platform centers on election reform (fair maps, open primaries, expanded voting access), ethics and campaign-finance oversight of lawmakers, and a balanced-market economic approach; as a Forward Party candidate she says she isn't bound to any single party platform.

Campaign finance is almost entirely self- and small-dollar-funded. Her 2026 Convention Report (1/1–3/30/26) was 100% self-funded — $386.96 total, all three contributions from Shelton herself, covering the filing fee and campaign setup costs. Her Primary Report (3/31–6/11/26) added $381.10 in real money from five individual donors ($41.50–$103 each), plus two in-kind gifts that don't count toward her cash total: $110.47 in T-shirts from a supporter, and a $2,365.00 voter-data package from the Forward Party of Utah — by far her largest contribution of any kind, but data/services from the state party rather than cash. YTD cash total: $768.06.

Two things worth noting for context: no general-period report has been filed yet — her most recent filing on record is the primary report ending 6/11/26, so any dollar comparison to Kyle (filed through 8/4/26) or James (filed through 8/21/26) is comparing different points in time, not a fundraising lull. And every single itemized contribution in both her reports was entered as an amendment, not in the original filing — the same "shell report, add real numbers later" pattern already documented for HD10's Jim Alvey. Legally compliant if each amendment lands within Utah's 31-day window, but it means her filings look current on the surface while the real contribution data arrives afterward. A genuine deletion also appears in her Primary Report: a $53.99 in-kind "door hangers" gift was fully wiped via amendment on 6/16/26.
