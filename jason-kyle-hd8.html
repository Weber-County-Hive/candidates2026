Case 02 · Utah House District 8 · Ogden Valley, Pleasant View, North Ogden & Mountain Green

Where HD-8's Money Comes From

All three candidates have filed their September 30th reports. Rep. Jason Kyle (R) raised most of his 2026 money from PACs, parties and businesses. Kim James (D) and Libby Shelton (Utah Forward Party) raised most of theirs from individuals. Grades, the full money picture and Kyle's bill record are below.

Updated Oct. 2, 2026, with legislative-record grades added. Earlier update Sept. 30, 2026, with all three candidates' September 30th filings (period ending Sept. 25) and their disclosure forms.

The three-way race: Jason Kyle (R, incumbent since 2023) vs. Kim James (D, retired United Methodist pastor) vs. Libby Shelton (Utah Forward Party). Kyle advanced from the 2026 Republican convention; James and Shelton also appear on the Weber County general-election ballot per the County Clerk's official candidate list.
What his campaign says vs. what the record shows: Kyle's campaign site (jasonbkyle.com) pledges he'll "fight against tax increases" and "protect your tax dollars." Going through his full sponsorship record and campaign finance history, we could not find a bill he authored, cosponsored, or voted on that actually delivers a tax cut or blocks a tax increase — and no donor pattern suggesting a specific tax fight he's waging on constituents' behalf. His one enacted tax bill (HB 300, below) is a school-district funding mechanism, not relief. Where this claim fits in his actual record is not something the public documents show; the bill docket below is the full record we found, for readers to check against the claim themselves.

Kyle (R): $9,120 this period, 11 of 16 gifts from groups

The largest gifts this period came from the Weber County Republican Party ($3,000), Google Client Services ($1,000), the Utah House Republican Election Committee ($1,000), U-Car PAC ($751) and Utah Medical PAC ($350). Five businesses and professional groups gave on Sept. 22: Cigna, NAIFA Utah IFAPAC, Paul Rogers & Associates and WCF Mutual ($250 each) and Parsons Behle & Latimer ($200). Individuals gave $1,569: Rep. Casey Snider of Paradise ($1,000), Adam Gardiner of West Jordan ($294.20), Cortlund Ashton of Ogden Valley ($250) and Sylvia Salisbury of Ogden ($25). CMV PAC also gave $537.82 in voter data, an in-kind gift. Earlier versions of this page said none of his 2026 donors were from Weber County; that was true through the primary, but this report includes the county Republican Party and two Weber County residents.

He spent $2,944 this period, mostly on signs (Renaissance Signs, $1,219) and printing (Salt Lake Mailing & Printing, $702), plus Facebook ads, supplies and travel.

James (D): $11,284 this period, mostly from individuals

About $9,131 came from roughly 60 individuals, many from Ogden, North Ogden, Eden and Huntsville. Repeat donors include Alan Livingston ($500 three times this period) and Jeanette Rodgers (several gifts of $100 to $200). Rosemary Lesser, the Democratic candidate in House District 10, gave $1,000. Groups gave $2,150: the Weber County Democratic Party ($2,000 on Sept. 25) and the Women's Democratic Club ($150).

She spent $19,140, and $15,930 of it (83%) went to Postcardmania for four rounds of mailers between Aug. 7 and Sept. 25. JM Alexander Corp received $1,625 for advertising, and Ogden Valley Adaptive Sports received $500 on June 30, listed as "Campaign Expense."

Shelton (UFP): no new cash this period

Shelton reported no cash contributions this period and $32.40 in spending on web hosting and bank fees. She reported two in-kind gifts: $53.99 in door hangers from MoveForwardWithUs and a $100 Tomato Days booth she paid for herself, marked as a loan that two supporters will repay. All $768 of her 2026 cash came from individuals, about half from Shelton herself. Her largest support all year was in-kind: a $2,365 voter-data package from the Forward Party of Utah in June.

Grades on the five questions

Every item is graded on the same five questions used across this project: Power, Transparency, Financial accountability, Environmental impact and Community impact. A C means the record meets what Utah law requires, the status quo. A B or A means the candidate went beyond it, and the record shows it. D means something is vague or bundled, or a public check is weakened; F means something required is missing, or a check is removed with nothing in its place. On reports, one required item missing is graded D, since it can be a mistake; several missing from the same report is graded F. On disclosure forms, an item voters can't see anywhere else (a company, a board seat, a job) left off is graded D; two left off, D−; three or more, F, a pattern. Legislative pay, which is public, left off with nothing else missing is graded D+. Anything named somewhere on the form counts as listed, and a form not on file is graded F. (Disclosure-form ladder added Oct. 3, 2026.) How a grade can change: candidates can file an amended report or an updated disclosure form at any time. When the missing or unclear item is fixed on the public record, we update the grade and note the date. Feedback appreciated: candidates and readers can send corrections or records we missed to webercountyhive@gmail.com, and we check every one against the public record.

Overall is the average of the graded questions (A = 4, B = 3, C = 2, D = 1, F = 0; N/A and dashes are left out). A plus or minus shows where the average falls between letters, and an average exactly halfway counts toward the higher letter with a minus. Added Oct. 2, 2026.

The other candidates

Kim James (D)

Retired United Methodist pastor (Ogden First UMC, 2013–2025), 40 years in ministry across Colorado and Utah, lives on Ogden's East Bench; now a part-time chaplain at Ogden Regional Medical Center. Her campaign is funded mostly by individuals, including her own gifts (she files as Kimal James) and gifts from "Oscar James" at the same address and a small out-of-state donor base consistent with a long ministry career. Her group money in 2026: UINPAC (Utah Investment Network PAC) $7,500 in the convention period, the Weber County Democratic Party $2,000, Teamsters Local 222 $750 and the Women's Democratic Club $300. Raised $32,143 in 2026 through Sept. 25, per the state's data export of her filings.

Libby Shelton (Utah Forward Party)

Full name per her own site: Libby Hernandez Shelton (CFIS/COI filings use Elizabeth Anne Shelton). Weber County resident 20+ years; works as a therapist specializing in substance use and domestic violence treatment (MSW, Utah State University; BA Anthropology, Weber State University). Politically active for 15 years as a state/county caucus delegate, including a decade as her local caucus's vice chair before switching to the Forward Party in 2025. Currently Weber County Vice-Chair of the Forward Party of Utah — a local party officer, not just a candidate. Platform centers on election reform (fair maps, open primaries, expanded voting access), ethics and campaign-finance oversight of lawmakers, and a balanced-market economic approach; as a Forward Party candidate she says she isn't bound to any single party platform.

Campaign finance is almost entirely self- and small-dollar-funded. Her 2026 Convention Report (1/1–3/30/26) was 100% self-funded — $386.96 total, all three contributions from Shelton herself, covering the filing fee and campaign setup costs. Her Primary Report (3/31–6/11/26) added $381.10 in real money from five individual donors ($41.50–$103 each), plus two in-kind gifts that don't count toward her cash total: $110.47 in T-shirts from a supporter, and a $2,365.00 voter-data package from the Forward Party of Utah — by far her largest contribution of any kind, but data/services from the state party rather than cash. YTD cash total: $768.06 (unchanged through Sept. 25; see the September 30th section above).

Two notes on her filings. Most of her itemized entries in the state's data file are marked as amendments rather than original entries. And a $53.99 in-kind door-hanger gift from MoveForwardWithUs, first dated April 29, was removed by amendment on June 16; the same gift, same amount and description, appears again in her September 30th period dated Aug. 1. Earlier versions of this page described it only as removed.
