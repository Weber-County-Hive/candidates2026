Bar Discipline History

New Jersey Disciplinary Review Board, October 25, 2021: Joseph Edwyn Kerry received a Letter of Admonition (Docket No. DRB 21-159) for practicing law in Utah while unlicensed. He established and operated Peace Legal, PLLC in Utah from 2017–2020 without a Utah license, billing Utah clients and maintaining sole control of the firm's financial accounts.

Kerry has been licensed in New Jersey and Pennsylvania since 1997. The Board cited mitigating factors: expressed remorse, no additional violations, the firm was closed, no clients harmed, and an "unblemished disciplinary record of more than twenty years" prior. The result was an admonition (the lowest level of formal attorney discipline).

Relevance: A sitting USBE member who chairs the committee overseeing teacher licensing and discipline has his own bar-discipline history for a licensing-related violation (unauthorized practice)—directly analogous subject matter.

Why It Matters

Utah's State Board of Education makes decisions affecting millions of students, teachers, charter schools, and taxpayers. Its members operate in a system where campaign donors may simultaneously be regulated stakeholders, and where nonprofit advocates may be colleagues, endorsers, or organizational partners.

Those relationships aren't automatically corrupt. They become a public-trust problem when they're difficult to see—when disclosure forms omit major roles, when nonprofit advocacy goes directly to a committee chaired by that nonprofit's own leader, and when deleted campaign contributions erase the full donor picture.

This article puts the relationships in one place: who contributed, who endorsed, who leads which organization, what those organizations advocate, and what the official disclosure doesn't clearly show. Voters can decide what weight each fact deserves. They shouldn't have to reconstruct the network themselves.
