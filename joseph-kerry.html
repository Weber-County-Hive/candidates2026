Utah State Board of Education, District 2

The Network Around Joseph Kerry

What public records show — and what voters should ask. Kerry chairs the board's Law and Licensing Committee, which oversees educator-licensing rules and the UPPAC discipline process. Campaign-finance reports, nonprofit tax filings, corporate registrations, court records and Kerry's own disclosures document a set of relationships surrounding that role.

Records reviewed through August 25, 2026 — campaign-finance filings, IRS Form 990 returns, Utah corporate registrations, court records, State Board minutes and Kerry's own disclosures. We report public records. You decide.

What this piece does not claim: the records do not prove a quid pro quo, misuse of office, or coordination among every person discussed here. A campaign contribution does not establish control over a candidate, and sharing a policy position does not establish improper conduct. The question is narrower — are voters seeing the full picture of the people and organizations surrounding a board member who helps write education rules?

A board member with direct licensing authority

Kerry's influence extends beyond an ordinary vote on the 15-member State Board. As chair of the Law and Licensing Committee, he helps manage the committee that evaluates educator-licensing rules and the UPPAC process. Full-board minutes show he has introduced licensing motions and participated in decisions accepting UPPAC recommendations to suspend or revoke educator licenses.

In April 2026, Kerry publicly argued that the UPPAC system needed reform. The Salt Lake Tribune reported that a paper he prepared described an educator-disciplinary process he believed treated teachers unfairly — a structural and procedural critique, not an accusation against UPPAC staff. That policy position is also the point where Kerry's public role intersects most directly with his outside relationships.

The charter-school executive who contributed to Kerry

Carolyn Sharette is chief executive of American Preparatory Schools, the charter-management organization overseeing American Preparatory Academy campuses, which the network says serves more than 5,000 Utah students. Sharette's own professional biography describes managing annual school budgets exceeding $65 million and advocating for school autonomy, parental choice and related legislation.

Those contributions are legal and do not prove Kerry acted for Sharette or American Preparatory. They establish that the leader of a company financially dependent on public charter-school contracts helped finance a candidate for the state body that writes education rules and maintains a liaison relationship with the State Charter School Board.

Sharette is not a politically neutral administrator. American Preparatory's 2021 statement on Critical Race Theory, written under her name, argued that related state training crossed into the politicization of K–12 education. Voters may agree or disagree with that position — the relevant fact is that the donor has identifiable policy interests before the system Kerry helps govern.

A court judgment involving the same management company

Sharette's campaign contributions must be kept legally separate from this civil judgment. Nothing in the case accused Kerry of misconduct or connected the lawsuit to campaign money or State Board decisions.

Following a four-day bench trial, Third District Court Judge Coral Sanchez found Sharette's testimony not credible and ruled that Sharette and other defendants had improperly obtained her cognitively impaired sister's 49.25% interest in American Preparatory Schools without compensation.

Findings of the September 19, 2024 order
Breach of fiduciary duty, constructive fraud, fraudulent nondisclosure and concealment, securities fraud, financial exploitation of a vulnerable adult, unjust enrichment and conversion. The court found the resulting damages arose from conduct that was “willful and malicious or intentionally fraudulent,” or that showed knowing and reckless disregard for another person's rights. Total damages exceeded $9 million before attorney fees and interest, including $2.98 million in punitive damages.

These findings arose from a private ownership dispute. They do not establish wrongdoing by American Preparatory Academy's public charter schools, Kerry, or any campaign. Their relevance is limited but real: they concern control of the private company that manages a large, publicly funded charter network while its chief executive contributes to State Board candidates. This article reports the September 2024 trial-court order and should be updated against later appellate, amended-judgment or settlement records.

Path Forward Utah: an outside leadership role

Path Forward Utah is an Ogden-based nonprofit founded by Jamie Renda, whose public materials describe her as a conservative civic advocate focused on education, government accountability, school choice and community engagement. IRS filings show Kerry has held an officer position throughout most of his State Board service.

The most recent filing reports $166,972 in revenue, all classified as contributions, and $128,002 in expenses. Renda is listed as president, working 40 hours per week and receiving $60,000; Kerry is listed as chair, working one hour per week, unpaid. The filing does not identify the nonprofit's contributors, leaving voters unable to determine who financed its growth from $14,397 in 2023 to $166,972 in 2025.

Disclosure gap
Utah Code §20A-11-1604 requires State Board members to file a complete conflict-of-interest disclosure. The public versions of Kerry's disclosure reviewed for this article do not identify Path Forward Utah, even though the organization reports him as vice president and then chair. That omission does not by itself prove a statutory conflict of interest — Utah law principally defines that term around a direct financial benefit, and Kerry is unpaid — but it raises a question about whether the form is complete under the disclosure rules that apply to unpaid leadership positions.

When the nonprofit's advocacy reached Kerry's committee

In December 2025, a Law and Licensing Committee meeting packet included a submission from Renda, using a Path Forward Utah email address, alleging evidentiary, structural-conflict and due-process problems in UPPAC proceedings. Kerry chaired the meeting at which the committee discussed the UPPAC process.

In March 2026, KSL identified Renda as Path Forward Utah's founder and a critic of UPPAC operations; discussing legislation to change educator discipline, she called it the first of many changes needed to provide educators due process. The Salt Lake Tribune then reported in April that Kerry himself had prepared a report arguing UPPAC needed reform.

Agreement between Kerry and an organization he chairs is not inherently improper — advocacy groups commonly recruit officials who share their beliefs. The disclosure question matters because the same person occupied both roles: chair of the State Board committee examining UPPAC, and chair of an outside nonprofit actively pressing complaints about UPPAC. That overlap should have been easy for the public to identify without comparing IRS returns, meeting attachments and State Board forms one at a time.

Joe for Utah and the completeness of Kerry's disclosure

Utah corporate records list Kerry as registered agent and manager of Joe for Utah, LLC, formed March 7, 2022 and renewed in March 2026. Its certificate of organization states its purpose is to advocate for education by working with parents and teachers to create policies supporting Utah's youth — a purpose that extends beyond a generic holding company and overlaps with Kerry's public education role.

The public conflict statement reviewed for this article does not clearly identify Joe for Utah, LLC. As with Path Forward Utah, the record alone doesn't establish a prohibited financial conflict — it creates a disclosure question: why doesn't an active education-advocacy company managed by a State Board member appear clearly on the form meant to identify his outside interests?

Kerry's earlier legal-practice admonition

Kerry is an attorney licensed outside Utah. He formed Peace Legal, PLLC in Ogden in 2017 and voluntarily dissolved it in 2020.

New Jersey Disciplinary Review Board, October 2021
Issued Kerry a formal admonition for unauthorized practice of law, finding he had established and operated Peace Legal in Utah despite not being licensed to practice law in the state. The firm's website used only a Utah address and phone number, Kerry billed Utah clients, and he held sole authority over the firm's financial accounts.

The board imposed its lowest formal level of discipline, expressly weighing that Kerry expressed remorse, no clients were harmed, Peace Legal had closed, and he had maintained an otherwise unblemished disciplinary record for more than 20 years. This was a professional-discipline matter, not a criminal case, and it predates Kerry's State Board term. It is relevant to a candidate whose committee responsibilities include professional licensing, conduct standards and due process.

A shared policy direction — not proof of a coordinated bloc

The people and organizations in these records don't speak with one voice on every issue. Their public statements nevertheless reveal substantial areas of agreement:

  • Expanding charter schools, school choice and institutional autonomy
  • Emphasizing parental authority in education
  • Resisting Critical Race Theory and related ideological instruction
  • Limiting bureaucratic or centralized control over schools
  • Reforming educator discipline to provide stronger procedural protections

Sharette publicly advocates charter autonomy and school choice. Renda and Path Forward Utah have advocated UPPAC reform and education choice. Kerry has argued for UPPAC reform and occupies a State Board position capable of turning policy ideas into rules and official recommendations. That's evidence of ideological alignment — not proof of conspiracy, secret coordination or improper influence.

What the records do — and do not — establish

The records establish
  • Sharette contributed personally to Kerry's campaigns while leading a company that manages publicly funded charter schools
  • A Utah court entered extensive civil findings against Sharette in a private dispute over ownership of that management company
  • Kerry served as officer and chair of Path Forward Utah while chairing the committee examining issues the nonprofit actively advocates about
  • Renda endorsed Kerry before later serving as the nonprofit's paid president while Kerry served as its unpaid chair
  • Kerry manages an active education-advocacy LLC not clearly identified on his public conflict statement
  • New Jersey's attorney-disciplinary board admonished Kerry in 2021 for operating a Utah law firm while unlicensed in the state
The records do not establish
  • That Sharette bought or controlled Kerry's vote
  • That Kerry intervened for American Preparatory Schools
  • That Path Forward Utah's contributors directed Kerry's State Board actions
  • That Kerry profited from his unpaid nonprofit role
  • That the Sharette civil judgment involved Kerry or campaign contributions
  • That everyone who shares these policy positions is part of a formally coordinated organization

Why it matters

Utah's State Board of Education makes decisions affecting students, teachers, charter schools and taxpayers statewide. Its members operate in a system where campaign donors may also be regulated stakeholders, and where nonprofit advocates may simultaneously be colleagues, endorsers or organizational partners. Those relationships aren't automatically corrupt. They become a public-trust problem when they're difficult to see.

This article isn't meant to tell voters that every relationship is disqualifying. It puts the relationships in one place: who contributed, who endorsed, who leads which organization, what those organizations advocate, and what the official disclosure doesn't clearly show. Voters can decide what weight each fact deserves — they shouldn't have to reconstruct the network themselves.
