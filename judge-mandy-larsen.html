Hive judge-rubric grades

How to read the grades: A C means the record meets what Utah law requires, the status quo. A B or A means the judge went beyond it, and the record shows it. D means the judge passed JPEC’s minimum but JPEC rates that area below the judge’s peers; F means below JPEC’s legal minimum score of 3.6. Every grade is taken from JPEC’s own published wording, the same way for all 57 judges. No grade is based on how any case came out, or on who appointed the judge.

What JPEC’s report says

The text below is JPEC’s own public report, reproduced as published. Quotes inside it are from JPEC’s survey respondents and courtroom observers, not from the Hive.

JPEC summary

Survey Respondent Recommendation:94% of survey respondents recommend Judge Larsen for retention

Performance Measures: Survey scores are analyzed for statistical significance to determine if the judge scored well above, above, consistent with, below, or well below their district court peers. Above Peers: Integrity & Judicial Temperament, Administrative Skills, Procedural Fairness

Consistent with Peers: Legal Ability

Respondent and Observer Feedback:Respondents say that Judge Larsen knows the law and applies it fairly.

They, along with courtroom observers, applaud her sincere respect for all participants, her careful attentiveness, and her ability to explain information in a straightforward manner.

Observers further appreciate the judge’s display of neutrality.

All four observers report confidence that they would be treated fairly if they were to appear in Judge Larsen’s court.

Courtroom observers, overall

  • Together, the four observers were enthusiastically positive about Judge Larsen.
  • All observers reported that if they appeared before Judge Larsen they would be treated fairly.

What observers agreed on

  • Observers variously reported that Judge Larsen presented as patient, professional, and respectful, and she greeted, dismissed, and apologized in a manner that “displayed authentic respect for all parties.” She showed consideration of all parties’ needs and interests and, in one hearing, she “automatically slowed down and spoke closer to the microphone” when a defendant revealed they were hard of hearing. She took time to act in a measured way across cases, and “language issues were dealt with carefully,” postponing one case four weeks so that an interpreter could be present. The judge appeared intentional when giving participants a voice in court, and one observer appreciated her saying to a defendant, “I would be glad to hear anything you want to say.” She provided clear communication by explaining the process for defendants, and she “frequently ensured parties’ comprehension of the proceedings.”
  • All observers particularly highlighted the judge’s neutrality. They variously described her as impartial, balanced, and consistent. One reported that she appeared “neutral in reading rights, articulating charges, and screening for public defense qualification,” and a second found that in a difficult to hear child abuse case, the judge made no statement or revealed no “body language to show any pre-judgment of the parties.” Another indicated that “all defendants were informed of their right to due process.”

Other observations

  • Two observers stated that the judge listened well, with one describing her listening as excellent and the other finding that she “listened closely.”
  • Two agreed that the judge seemed organized and efficient. One said that she “conducted proceedings with efficiency” and the other noted that she was “very aware and knowledgeable about the cases” and “ran their courtroom with a strong sense of structure.”

Biography (from JPEC)

Judge Mandy Larsen was appointed to the Sixth District Court by Governor Spencer Cox and took the oath of office in April 2022. She serves Sevier, Sanpete, Wayne, Piute, Garfield, and Kane counties. Judge Larsen received a law degree from the J. Reuben Clark Law School at Brigham Young University in 2003. She was a member of the Education and Law Journal and a member of the NALSA moot court team. After law school, she was a law clerk / bailiff to Commissioner Patton and Judge Donald Eyre, Jr at the Fourth District Court. Judge Larsen started her own law firm in 2004 with an office sharing arrangement at the law firm Esplin | Weight in Provo, Utah where she practiced primarily in the areas of criminal defense, family law, probate law, and juvenile law. Judge Larsen also handled several criminal defense contracts. In 2008, Judge Larsen relocated her practice to Richfield, Utah where she held a public defense contract with Sevier County. In 2009, Judge Larsen also began working as a certified mediator. In 2014, Judge Larsen began working as a prosecutor for the Sevier County Attorney’s Office and Gunnison City.
